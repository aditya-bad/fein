## 1. Thesis in one line
Method-test thesis: Uttam Sugar Mills crushes cane at four mills (27,000 TCD: Barkatpur 8,500, Shermau 6,000, Khaikheri 5,500 in western UP; Libberheri 7,000 in Uttarakhand), sells sugar (FY26 36.8 lakh qtl at Rs 4,143/qtl, incl. a branded/specialty line), ethanol/ENA (847 lakh BL from 300 KLPD of molasses distilleries + a 40 KLPD grain distillery at 83.7%-owned UDL) and surplus power (1,006 lakh kWh to UPPCL/UPCL). Every major price is administered: cane cost is the state-set SAP (Rs 390-405/qtl for SS 2025-26, ~74% of capacity in UP), sugar has a Rs 31/kg MSP frozen since 2019 plus monthly release quotas and an export ban (13-May-2026), and ethanol is sold to OMCs at government-fixed feedstock prices. At a 9.42% recovery the SAP alone costs ~Rs 4,150 per qtl of sugar - about the sugar price - so profit comes from by-products and recovery. Group ROIC ~9% on seasonal-average capital (full-cycle ROIIC ~5.6%) is below a 12.5% cost of equity; EPS FY17 27.95 -> FY26 26.30.

## 2. Variant view (what we believe that the price does not, and why we could know it)
Market: at Rs 348.50 (mcap Rs 1,329 cr) the stock trades at 14.7x reported TTM EPS (23.72) and 15.2x core / 10-year-average EPS (22.9-23.0) - HOT vs its own 10-year FY-end median (8.0x; 90th percentile) and NORMAL vs UP-sugar peers (Balrampur 31x, Triveni 15.5x, Dhampur 11.4x). The price rose from ~Rs 187 (2026 low) to 389 in 2026 on a domestic sugar-price spike (Rs 48 -> 56/kg Jul-Aug 2026, stock limits imposed) despite H1 FY27 PBT -62% after a flood-hit crush (-25%). Our read: no mispricing we can claim; the price already capitalises a good 2026-27 season. (1) Core EPS ~= reported (other income 5% of PBT, exceptionals -1.35 cr) - L44 check passes, unlike SHK. (2) Group ROIC ~9% and ROIIC ~5.6% < CoE, so the DCF is near zero and every scenario above 1x is an exit-multiple bet (L21); our base exit 9x (FY22-26 median) implies de-rating from 15x. (3) A material government-input change is missing from the company's own disclosures (L40): the ESY 2026-27 OMC tender (BPCL, 16-Oct-2026) seeks no ethanol from cane juice, syrup or B-heavy molasses, while three UP units ran on B-heavy and Barkatpur on syrup in SS 2025-26 and the 300 KLPD rating is 'based on B-heavy/syrup'; the 31-Oct-2026 results deck does not mention it. (4) The 2026-27 UP SAP is not yet announced (farmers ask Rs 500 vs 390-400). Evidence for the other side: ratings upgraded twice (CARE A Stable Sep-2025; India Ratings A Stable Jun-2026), debt falls to ~Rs 30 cr net each September, recovery improving (9.42% vs 8.94%), C-heavy ethanol stays eligible and lost B-heavy diversion returns as extra sugar into a tight market (closing stock ~4.25 mt), and an MSP hike (ISMA asks Rs 40-41/kg) would lift every miller. Departure from the outside view: raw P(blow-up) 10% < class 15% (A-rated, no pledge) and raw P(<=0.5x) far above 11% because the price sits at 1.9x the own-history P/E median; default w=0.5. **Outside-view anchoring (v1.2, L36):** anchored P = 0.50 × base rate + 0.50 × model for P(≤0.5x) and P(blow-up) (weight: stated). The expected-value gate and Kelly use the anchored tails; the paper calls keep the raw model probability (with the anchored one alongside), so Brier scoring can tell which was better. This is a calibration safeguard, not a change to the hurdle. The default weight is 0.5; a lower weight needs a written evidence note, never below 0.25.

> **WARNING: anchoring lowered documented tails — check evidence note** (P(≤0.5x): the class base rate is below our model tail, so anchoring made the card more optimistic, L40). If the model tail rests on company-specific evidence (a lease expiry, a licence dispute, a debt wall), the raw tail is the honest number: write that evidence in the deep dive and size off the raw tail by hand; the mechanical size below uses the anchored one.

## 9. Pre-mortem ("it is 2031 and this went badly; why?")
1. The 2026-27 crop came back big, sugar prices fell back towards the frozen MSP, the UP government still raised the SAP before the 2027 state election, and B-heavy ethanol stayed excluded: margins returned to the FY18/FY20 pattern (EPS Rs 4-14) and the P/E reverted to the 4-8x own range.
2. Ethanol allocation litigation and the dedicated-ethanol-plant preference left the 250 KLPD Barkatpur distillery running on own C-heavy molasses at well under half capacity; UDL's grain economics depended on FCI rice prices that rose.
3. A promoter-family change (the ~19% of promoter shares that never vote) or a large related-party capex order to the promoter's engineering companies diverted cash.
