## 1. Thesis in one line
Method-test thesis: S H Kelkar (Keva) is India's largest Indian-origin fragrance-and-flavour compounder (FY26 revenue Rs 2,368 cr: Fragrance 90%, Flavours 10%), selling blends to FMCG companies and blenders from Mumbai/Raigad (Mulund, Vashivali, Vanavate, Mahad), Vapi, Indonesia, the Netherlands (Holland Aromatics, Almere), Italy (CFF) and new creative centres in Germany, the UK and the US. Gross margin is steady at ~42-43%, but EBITDA margin fell from 16% (FY24) to 10% (FY26) and 12.9% (H1 FY27) as Rs ~80 cr/yr of creative-centre opex, higher insurance after the Apr-2024 Vashivali fire and FX losses hit; consolidated EPS was Rs 5.05 in FY16 and Rs 5.00 in FY26 while invested capital roughly 2.6x. The 5-year outcome depends on whether ~Rs 2,330 cr of invested capital (55% of non-current assets now in Europe) can earn more than the ~3% full-cycle ROIIC of the last eight years, with net debt of Rs 911 cr and interest cover of 2.4x.

## 2. Variant view (what we believe that the price does not, and why we could know it)
Market: at Rs 127.53 the stock trades at 18.7x reported trailing EPS (Rs 6.82) and 19.1x 10-year average EPS - COOL vs its own 10-year FY-end median (23.9x) and COLD vs ingredient peers (Privi 37x, Galaxy 30x). Our read: the cheapness is mostly earnings quality and returns, not mispricing. (1) Rs 63 cr of TTM pre-tax profit is fire-insurance realisations booked as exceptional items; on core earnings (EBIT ex other income Rs 145 cr, effective tax ~35-51% because foreign losses are unshielded, interest Rs 61 cr) EPS is ~Rs 3.4-3.9 and the P/E ~33-37x (screener's ex-exceptional 32.5x agrees). (2) Group ROIC is ~4.4% (6.8% if the Rs 80 cr of creative-centre opex is added back, as management suggests) and full-cycle ROIIC ~3% against a 12.5% cost of equity; at those returns the worksheet's DCF value is below net debt, so every scenario above 1x is an exit-multiple bet (L21). (3) Management guided 15% EBITDA for FY26 (May-2025) and 14-15% for H2 FY26 (Nov-2025) and delivered 10.2% and 9.4%; plant dates slipped about a year (Vanavate: 'later this calendar year' in May-2025 -> Q4 FY27). Hand-ledger credibility 31% vs a 60% peer median. Evidence for the other side: Flavours earns ~27% pre-tax on segment assets and grew 47% in H1 FY27; H1 FY27 EBITDA +33%; gross margin held through an input-cost spike; Holland Aromatics and the creative centres are 'all cost in' with first sales in the US, UK and Germany; the CEO promises 15%+ EBITDA within 12-18 months and debt reduction from Mar-2027. No claimable edge: we cannot see client wins at the creative centres earlier than the market. The trackable question is dated and public: does H2 FY27 EBITDA reach ~14% and net debt fall below Rs 850 cr by Mar-2027? Departure from the outside view: raw P(<=0.5x) 17% is above the class 11% (leverage, sub-CoE returns, an 86% drawdown in 2016-20 without a revenue decline) and raw P(blow-up) 8% is below 15% (AA- rating, no pledge stress); default w=0.5 lowers the first and raises the second. **Outside-view anchoring (v1.2, L36):** anchored P = 0.50 × base rate + 0.50 × model for P(≤0.5x) and P(blow-up) (weight: stated). The expected-value gate and Kelly use the anchored tails; the paper calls keep the raw model probability (with the anchored one alongside), so Brier scoring can tell which was better. This is a calibration safeguard, not a change to the hurdle. The default weight is 0.5; a lower weight needs a written evidence note, never below 0.25.
