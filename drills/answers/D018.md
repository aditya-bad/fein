# D018 — Blind analyst answer (set 2)

## 1. Recognition declaration
**Recognised (~75%) as a small, century-old US diversified industrial with truck/vehicle accessories, security/locks and packaging-container units that made two acquisitions (I believe The Eastern Company). Exclude.** I have only a weak memory of its later share price.

## 2. Business understanding
- It is a collection of niche OEM component units with high-single-digit margins and ROCE of 8–12%. Inventory runs at ~100–120 days. Growth comes only from debt-funded acquisitions; organic revenue was flat for years.
- Cash conversion is good (FCF often above NI). Cash goes to a dividend paid for decades and now to paying down acquisition debt.

## 3. Bull / bear / variant
- **Bull:** FCF of ~42 is about a 9.5% yield on a market cap of 448. Debt can be paid down in about 5 years. Orders are recovering, and on Y-1 earnings it trades at ~14×.
- **Bear:** this is a mediocre-ROCE conglomerate. Recovery is already partly priced in (stock near the high of its five-year range; P/E 34× trough, 14× normal). Rising steel/aluminium and freight costs will squeeze its thin margins, and acquisitions bought at full prices dilute ROCE.
- **Variant view:** none. The likely outcome is an ordinary compounder that roughly tracks earnings.
- **Benign flags:** the trough P/E and the higher debt after the acquisition, which is serviceable given FCF.
- **Real flags:** no organic growth, and a ROCE that has never cleared ~12%.
- **Green flags I distrust:** the Y0 CFO, which was helped by a working-capital release.

## 4. Valuation
At normalised NI of ~30 and 14–16×, market cap is ~420–480, so the stock is fairly valued. A 3× move would need NI of ~90, which is not plausible.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 ≥3×: 0.04.**
- **O2 ≤0.5×: 0.12.** Leverage on a cyclical business, but the cash flow is real.
- **O3: 0.02.**

## 6. Decision
**AVOID (no edge, fair price), conviction 2.**
