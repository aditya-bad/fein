# D005 — Blind analyst answer (T = mid-March 2019)

## 1. Recognition
**Recognised as Sterling Construction (exclude).** A south-western heavy-civil contractor that bought a Sun Belt residential-foundations business in 2017, under an outside CEO hired in 2015, is identifiable. I know its later trajectory, so the drill is contaminated. The analysis below is from the packet only.

## 2. Business
- Heavy civil works on low-bid, fixed-price contracts: gross margins of 5–10%, operating margins of 2–5% for good operators, and large downside from a single bad bid (2013: −$70m).
- Profit drivers:
  1. Bid discipline and mix.
  2. Equipment utilisation.
  3. Specialty niches such as residential foundations, design-build, water and aviation, which earn mid-teens gross margins with less bid competition.
- Where cash goes: modest equipment capex (~1% of sales), working capital (billings vs costs), and acquisitions. NOLs shield taxes.

## 3. Cases and flags
- **Bull:**
  1. Clear evidence of a turnaround: gross margin 4.7% → 6.4% → 9.3% → 10.6%, and four straight quarters of rising operating income year on year.
  2. ROCE of ~17%, a record backlog of $851m (+14%), and FAST Act plus state fuel-tax funding.
  3. EV/EBITDA ~5.5x, P/E ~13.5x on effectively untaxed earnings, and net cash.
  4. The CEO has delivered on the margin plan he set.
  5. If the specialty share rises and EBITDA reaches $80–100m over 3–4 years at a 7–8x multiple, the market cap could be $600–800m+.
- **Bear:**
  1. Contractors are low-multiple businesses because bid blowups recur.
  2. The residential segment is exposed to a housing slowdown (rates up in late 2018).
  3. The NOLs will be used up, after which the tax rate normalises to ~25%.
  4. Shares were diluted 65% since 2012.
  5. The 2017 peak of $17–18 already reflected the optimism, and it didn't hold.
- **Variant perception:** the market still values it as a low-bid highway contractor. The mix shift means the business is becoming a specialty-services company that deserves a higher multiple. Earnings growth plus a re-rating is the twin-engine setup.
- **Flags I think are benign:**
  1. Past dilution, because it funded survival and the acquisition.
  2. A near-zero tax rate, which is real NOL value (a hidden asset), not low earnings quality.
  3. Goodwill of $85m, which comes from an acquisition with demonstrated margin accretion.
- **Red flags I keep:** single-state concentration, the 2013 history, and the fact that NCI takes a growing share of profit.
- **Green flags I distrust:** backlog quality (low-bid backlog can carry losses), and cash of $94m, partly in JVs.

## 4. Valuation
- At $335m market cap, the price implies roughly flat EBITDA (~$60m) and a contractor multiple.
- Fair value: $10–22. A 3x ($37.5, ~$1bn market cap) needs EPS of ~$2.5–3 and a 13–15x multiple. That's possible if specialty work grows to around half of profit.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 = 0.22.** Above the base rate: a proven turnaround, a cheap multiple, tax shields, funding tailwinds and a credible CEO.
- **O2 = 0.15.** Net cash and a diversified backlog. The main risk is a housing downturn plus a bad bid.
- **O3 = 0.04.** Net cash, a clean audit and effective ICFR.

## 6. Decision
**OWN** (satellite). Conviction 3.
