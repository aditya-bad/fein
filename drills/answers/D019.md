# D019 — Blind analyst answer (set 2)

## 1. Recognition declaration
**Recognised (~70%) as a small Indian folding-carton (printed paperboard) plus flexible-packaging converter serving FMCG brand owners, thinly held (I believe TCPL Packaging). Exclude.** I have a rough memory that the stock did very well afterwards, which contaminates any probability I give.

## 2. Business understanding
- It converts paperboard and film into printed packs for large consumer-goods customers on multi-year relationships. Volume grows with consumption, and organised players are gaining share. Margins depend on passing through paperboard prices, with a lag.
- Profit was held back by new-plant depreciation and interest: EBITDA grew from ~94 to ~150, but PAT stayed flat. Capex is now done, free cash flow turned positive (~62) and debt/equity is falling.

## 3. Bull / bear / variant
- **Bull:** revenue has grown 10–11% a year for six years without a down year, which shows a resilient customer base, including through the lockdown. The capex cycle is complete, so EBITDA growth now flows to equity. Interest falls as debt is repaid and depreciation growth slows. If revenue grows 12% a year to ~1,750 at 16% EBITDA, EBITDA is ~280 and PAT is ~120–130 by Y5. At 18–20×, market cap is ~2,300, about 4× today's 540. Paper replacing plastic, global brand owners moving their sourcing, and exports are tailwinds that can be checked from outside. Thin following, with 6.8k shareholders.
- **Bear:** customer concentration, volatile paperboard prices (Y-3 dip), debt/equity still at 1.1, and family control. Management could restart capex.
- **Variant view:** the market is reading flat PAT as "no growth" when it actually reflects a capex J-curve. This is the L12 false-alarm pattern: negative FCF in the build phase, a mechanism outsiders can verify (plants built, volumes in essential goods) and cash now arriving.
- **Benign flags:** past negative FCF and high debt/equity, which are explained by completed capex. The Y-1 zero values are a source-data gap, not a company issue.
- **Real flags:** customer concentration and the pass-through lag.
- **Green flags I distrust:** management's "no large new capex" statement.

## 4. Valuation
A P/E of 15×, EV/EBITDA of ~6× and an FCF yield of ~11.5%, for a business growing revenue at ~10%+. Fair value is ~1.5–2× the current price on visible deleveraging alone.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 ≥3×: 0.33.** Operating and financial leverage plus a re-rating from a low base, with cash arriving.
- **O2 ≤0.5×: 0.08.** Essential-goods demand and a cheap starting multiple.
- **O3: 0.02.**
