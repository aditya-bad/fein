# D012 — Blind analyst answer (set 2)

## 1. Recognition declaration
**Recognised as a US marketer of children's books sold through a home-based consultant network: Educational Development Corp, high confidence. Exclude.** The giveaways: a non-manufacturer with a wide catalogue and ~10 months of inventory, one dominant supplier, a headquarters/warehouse bought with a mortgage, a 2-for-1 split, and "schools disrupted" driving the pandemic surge. I also recall roughly how it turned out (badly after the pandemic), so this answer is contaminated.

## 2. Business understanding
- It buys finished product from a dominant supplier at ~30% of the selling price, so gross margin is ~70%. Consultant commissions are the main operating cost. Profit depends on the number of active consultants times sales per consultant, so it moves with recruiting.
- Inventory is ~315 days of COGS and is the main use of cash. The building and warehouse automation are the other capital needs. Receivables are close to zero because consultants pay at order.

## 3. Bull / bear / variant
- **Bull:** a P/E of 7.4× and EV/EBITDA of 5.5×; debt fell to a debt/equity of 0.27; ROCE of 35%; a family owns ~23% and its interests line up with shareholders; it pays a dividend. If half of the pandemic consultants stay, earnings settle well above the pre-pandemic level.
- **Bear:** revenue grew 81% from a one-off recruiting surge driven by people stuck at home with schools shut. The packet itself notes that sales-force businesses usually fall the year after a surge. Inventory was built deliberately into a demand peak, and the warehouse capex was sized for peak volume. Freight costs are rising. If revenue goes back to ~40–45, net income could fall to ~1–2, the P/E on that is ~15–30×, and the inventory has to be funded with debt as rates rise. Concentration on one supplier is a further risk.
- **Variant view:** the low P/E is a peak-earnings trap. A 52-week low at T shows the market already doubts the surge, but the business has not yet started to decline.
- **Benign flags:** the high inventory days are a structural feature of a wide catalogue and were stable for years, and the small regional auditor fits a small company.
- **Real flags:** the inventory build into a peak, capacity capex sized for peak volume, and channel churn.
- **Green flags I distrust:** the 35% ROCE and the 7× P/E, both measured on a surge year.

## 4. Valuation
At mid-cycle net income of ~2.0 (the Y-2/Y-1 average) and 12×, market cap would be ~24 against 30 today. Net income would have to hold at ~4 for the stock to be cheap. Fair value is about the current price or lower, with a skew to the downside.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 ≥3×: 0.05.** The surge would have to prove permanent and keep growing; channel economics argue against it.
- **O2 ≤0.5×: 0.45.** A post-surge consultant decline plus an inventory and debt overhang is the base case for this business model.
- **O3: 0.08.** Debt-funded inventory with falling sales could lead to covenant stress; no fraud signals.

## 6. Decision
**AVOID, conviction 3.**
