# D009 — Blind analyst answer (T = mid-July 2019)

## 1. Recognition
**Recognised as Jash Engineering (exclude).** An SME-listed water-gate maker that bought a century-old developed-market gate brand in 2017 is identifiable. I have memory of its later trajectory, so the drill is contaminated. The analysis below is from the packet only.

## 2. Business
- It makes engineered-to-order fabricated and cast equipment: gates, valves and screens. Specification and references win tenders. Pricing is project-by-project, and gross margins are decent in a niche with few certified makers.
- Long lead times create inventory and debtor days of ~110–250. These are largely funded by supplier credit and customer advances (payables ~180 days).
- Profit drivers:
  1. Order inflow, especially exports of the acquired brand made at Indian cost: a labour and fabrication cost arbitrage of perhaps 30–50% against a developed-market plant.
  2. Factory utilisation.
- Where cash goes: capex for capacity (~₹17–24 cr a year recently) and working capital.

## 3. Cases and flags
- **Bull:**
  1. A cost-arbitrage moat. The 100-year-old brand gives access to municipal specifications in a developed market with an ageing-water-infrastructure replacement cycle, while Indian water missions also tender modern gates.
  2. FY19 revenue grew 42%, and the margin recovered from 5% to 9%.
  3. If the margin returns to 15–17% (the FY15–16 level) on ₹350–400 cr of revenue in 3–4 years, EBITDA is ~₹55–65 cr. At 10–12x that is an EV of ₹600–750 cr against ₹215 cr today, so a 3x+ on equity is plausible.
- **Bear:** this is a micro-cap with thin liquidity on an SME platform. Execution and integration risk is high, revenue is lumpy and government customers pay slowly. The FY18 collapse shows earnings fragility. A promoter of 54% with no pledge is good, but there's little float.
- **Variant perception:** the market values it as an illiquid SME fabricator. The real asset is an export platform with a brand.
- **Flags I think are benign:**
  1. Debtor days of ~110–125 and inventory of ~190–250 days, which are normal for engineered-to-order project equipment and offset by payables, so net working capital is modest.
  2. The FY18 profit collapse, which has a specific, verifiable cause (relocation and integration of the acquired product) and reversed the next year.
  3. The high FY18 tax rate (72%), from subsidiary losses that can't be offset.
- **Red flags I keep:**
  1. Other income is rising (₹7 cr against PBT of ₹14 cr), so its quality and source need checking.
  2. CFO was negative in FY18.
  3. A ~₹20 cr IPO followed by steadily rising debt.
- **Green flags I distrust:** "growing order book" without a number.

## 4. Valuation
- EV of ₹215 cr at 9.5x EBITDA. The price implies FY19 margins persist with modest growth.
- Fair value: ₹100–250. The illiquidity discount is warranted.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 = 0.25.** A niche with an export cost-arbitrage engine, a trough-recovery margin and a small base. Micro-cap re-rating optionality, including migration to the main board.
- **O2 = 0.22.** Execution and illiquidity risk, and one bad year halves an SME stock.
- **O3 = 0.05.** Modest leverage, no pledge and an explained profit dip.

## 6. Decision
**WATCH**, leaning OWN (small satellite) if order-book numbers and FY20 margins confirm. Conviction 3.
