# D014 — Blind analyst answer (set 2)

## 1. Recognition declaration
**Recognised (~70% confident) as an Indian specialty-chemicals (personal-care ingredients) plus generic-pharma company that was doing an "asset-light" JV/divestment restructuring (I believe Vivimed Labs). Exclude.** The giveaways: a chemicals-plus-healthcare mix, a 90% fall from peak, JV restructuring and inventory of ~400 days. I have a vague memory that it went into debt distress later.

## 2. Business understanding
- It is a mix of small-volume specialty ingredients and generics. Revenue has not grown in six years, and EBITDA fell from 16–28% to 1%.
- Cash is trapped in inventory (~400 days) and serviced by stretching suppliers (payables up from 107 to 180–207 days). Interest of 26 is far above EBITDA of ~4.5, so the business cannot service its debt from operations.

## 3. Bull / bear / variant
- **Bull:** a P/B of 0.2. If asset sales cut debt and margins go back to 15% on ~500 of revenue, EBITDA is ~75 and the equity is a leveraged option, so a market cap of 45 could multiply. The pharma rally and the pandemic tailwind help.
- **Bear:** asset sales over four years did not cut consolidated debt, which means proceeds were absorbed elsewhere or the deals were not cash-generative. Inventory of ~400 days and stretched payables are a classic sign of earnings-quality problems or channel stuffing. The Y-3 profit spike came from one-offs. The promoter stake is falling, perhaps through pledge invocation, which is unknown. Credit is tight.
- **Variant view:** none worth taking. The "asset-light" story can only be verified from company data, which is the L12 trap pattern.
- **Benign flags:** none convincing. The lockdown explains a few weeks of Y0, not a margin collapse from 14% to 1%.
- **Real flags:** interest cover below 1×, ~400 inventory days, debt not falling despite asset sales, a declining promoter stake, unknown audit opinion, and a stake of 45 against debt of 545.
- **Green flags I distrust:** the Y-1 CFO of 181 (it came from a payables stretch) and the P/B of 0.2 (book value is probably overstated by inventory and intangibles).

## 4. Valuation
EV is ~590 against EBITDA of ~4.5, which is meaningless. Equity is a deep out-of-the-money option on a restructuring that would have to succeed. Fair value is near zero unless the debt is restructured in shareholders' favour.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 ≥3×: 0.07.** Small option value from a lottery-ticket stub in a pharma bull market.
- **O2 ≤0.5×: 0.65.** Distress is the most likely path.
- **O3: 0.45.** Debt cannot be serviced from EBITDA; default, a one-time settlement, an insolvency process or a forced restructuring is likely, and fraud findings are plausible given the working-capital pattern.

## 6. Decision
**AVOID, conviction 5.**
