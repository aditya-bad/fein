# D008 — Blind analyst answer (T = end May 2019)

## 1. Recognition
**Recognised as Sical Logistics (exclude).** A logistics company in a group whose consumer-business flagship faced a 2017 tax raid and a 2019 asset sale is identifiable. I know the post-T outcome, so the drill is contaminated. The analysis below is from the packet only.

## 2. Business
- This is asset-heavy logistics: port terminals, rail rakes, CFS, mining-evacuation equipment and dredgers.
- Each asset needs heavy upfront capex and earns low-teens EBITDA margins once utilised. Government contracts pay slowly.
- Profit drivers: asset utilisation and volume ramp, against fixed depreciation and interest.
- Where cash goes: endless capex (cumulative investing outflow FY15–19 of ~₹970 cr against CFO of ~₹380 cr) and interest. The gap is funded by bank debt and promoter-group unsecured loans.

## 3. Cases and flags
- **Bull:** the mining-services contract ramp and ~₹1,030 cr of CWIP eventually come on stream. If ROCE reaches 10–12% on ~₹2,000 cr of capital, EBIT is ~₹220 cr against interest of ~₹70 cr. The equity has high leverage to that.
- **Bear:**
  1. ROCE of 5–7% for five years, and CWIP that has grown every year without earning. That points either to stuck projects or to capitalising costs, which flatters P&L.
  2. Net debt/EBITDA ~8x and interest cover ~1.7x.
  3. A tax rate of 44%, which suggests losses in some subsidiaries that can't be offset (or disallowances).
  4. Promoter shares are heavily pledged, and the group is leveraged at the holding level, is under tax investigation and is selling assets to deleverage.
  5. The parent is lending to the company now, but a stressed parent will need cash back.
- **Variant perception:** none on the bull side. The equity is the most junior claim in a stressed group.
- **Flags I think are benign:** high debtor days on government contracts are normal in the sector. Negative FCF would be fine in a ramping infrastructure business if ROCE were rising, but it isn't, so I keep that flag.
- **Green flags I distrust:**
  1. 28–31% revenue growth, which comes from a single contract and adds working capital.
  2. EBITDA, because CWIP capitalisation may be inflating it.
  3. "Group deleveraging", because deleveraging at the parent doesn't help this subsidiary and may starve it.

## 4. Valuation
- EV ₹2,000 cr at 11.5x EBITDA, for an asset with 5% ROCE. Equity of ₹600 cr is a thin sliver on top of ~₹1,400 cr of debt.
- Fair value: ₹0–120. Expected value is well below the price.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 = 0.05.** Requires CWIP to come on stream fast and the group to stabilise.
- **O2 = 0.65.** Over-leverage, pledge-triggered selling and contagion from group stress.
- **O3 = 0.40.** Interest cover 1.7x, net debt/EBITDA 8x, an ever-growing CWIP, dependence on a stressed parent, heavy pledging and a group under investigation. That puts it well above the 3–8% base rate.

## 6. Decision
**AVOID.** Conviction 5.
