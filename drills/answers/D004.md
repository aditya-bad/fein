# D004 — Blind analyst answer (T = mid-July 2017)

## 1. Recognition
**Recognised as Kwality Ltd (exclude).** The north Indian bulk-ghee/B2B dairy profile with this revenue scale and the pledged single promoter identify it. I know the outcome, so the drill is contaminated. The analysis below is from the packet only.

## 2. Business
- It buys milk from farmers and collection agents, pays within days and converts the milk to ghee, powder and butter. Most of that is sold in bulk to B2B buyers and traders.
- Bulk dairy is a thin-spread, commodity business with 3–6% EBITDA margins. It needs working capital mainly for inventory (seasonal flush-season powder and butter stocks).
- **Unit-economics contradiction:**
  1. Real dairies carry high inventory and low receivables, because institutional buyers pay in 30–45 days and traders pay faster.
  2. This company shows debtor days of ~85–100, inventory days of only ~10–20 and payables of ~3–6 days.
  3. Its receivables (~₹1,600 cr) are larger than its net worth. The balance sheet doesn't match how the business physically works.

## 3. Cases and flags
- **Bull (the narrative):** a 6.5x revenue increase over FY10–17, a consumer-brand pivot with new plants, a P/E of ~18.5x against listed dairy peers on far higher multiples, and an Indian dairy market growing 6–8%.
- **Bear:**
  1. Over FY10–17, cumulative PAT was ~₹930 cr against cumulative CFO of −₹50 cr and FCF of −₹860 cr. Profits never turned into cash for seven years.
  2. Debt rose ₹1,440 cr.
  3. 44% of promoter shares are pledged.
  4. There were repeated preferential allotments of shares and warrants.
  5. The tax rate was ~2–15% for several years, low for a domestic dairy processor.
  6. The dividend payout is ~1%. A business genuinely earning ₹190 cr would pay more.
  7. Capex of ₹440 cr was capitalised in one year after CWIP sat for two years.
- **Variant perception:** the market is pricing a dairy growth story. I think the reported profits are probably not real cash profits. This is the most textbook profile in the set for "receivable-funded fake revenue".
- **Flags I think are benign:** few. The FY17 CFO improvement to ₹271 cr is a green flag but small. Low payables are explained by farmers being paid quickly, so that flag alone is benign. Combined with high receivables, it is damning.
- **Green flags I distrust:**
  1. ROCE of ~16–19%, which sits on unconverted receivables.
  2. Steady 6–7% margins with no commodity volatility, even though global milk-powder prices crashed in 2015–16. Real bulk dairies would have seen margin swings.

## 4. Valuation
- At EV ₹5,300 cr, the price implies the receivables are collectable and that ~₹190 cr of PAT is real.
- If receivables are impaired by 30–50%, net worth is wiped out.
- Fair value: ₹0–100, with a large chance of near zero. Even taken at face value, a pure bulk dairy with no cash conversion deserves 8–10x earnings, about ₹70–80.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 = 0.04.** It would need the brand pivot to work and the accounting to be clean.
- **O2 = 0.60.** Pledge-triggered selling plus any credit event.
- **O3 = 0.45.** Seven years of negative cumulative CFO against reported profit, receivables larger than net worth in a cash-paid input business, a large pledge and repeated allotments. Nearly every item in insight:forensic-redflag-checklist fires.

## 6. Decision
**AVOID.** Conviction 5.
