# D010 — Blind analyst answer (T = late March 2017)

## 1. Recognition
**Recognised as Francesca's Holdings (exclude).** ~670 small-format boutiques with this apparel/jewelry/gifts mix, a 2011 PE-backed IPO and the 2016 CEO departure identify it. I know the post-T outcome, so the drill is contaminated. The analysis below is from the packet only.

## 2. Business
- Small boutiques of ~1,400 sq ft (implied by ~$545/sq ft and ~$0.75m of sales per store).
- Unit economics are excellent at maturity:
  1. Low build-out capex (~$0.3–0.4m per store).
  2. Gross margins of ~47% (including occupancy).
  3. A "broad and shallow" assortment that limits markdowns.
- Profit drivers: comparable sales (high operating leverage, because occupancy is fixed), merchandise newness, and mall traffic.
- Where cash goes: new stores (~$22m capex) and large buybacks ($54m in FY16).

## 3. Cases and flags
- **Bull:** P/E ~14x, EV/EBIT ~7.7x, FCF yield ~8.5%, no debt and lean inventory. A new experienced CEO, an FY16 EPS of $1.09 that beat guidance, and a target of 900+ stores. If comps go positive, the operating margin recovers towards 18–20%.
- **Bear:**
  1. Sales per square foot fell from $632 to $545, and comps are weak (−2%, −5%, +3%, +2%, flat in Q4). Store growth of 9–10% a year is masking it, because new stores are smaller-volume or are cannibalising existing ones.
  2. Gross margin fell from ~53% to ~47%.
  3. Mall traffic is in secular decline. Off-balance-sheet leases (probably $250m+) are effectively debt, so equity holders are more levered than they look.
  4. Buybacks are reducing equity rather than funding resilience.
  5. CEO turnover, an interim CFO, and a 2012 CFO dismissal over disclosure.
  6. EPS growth in FY15–16 came partly from buybacks (share count −8%) and a one-off reversal of forfeited stock compensation.
- **Variant perception:** what looks like a cheap, cash-generative retailer is really an operating-leveraged fixed-lease business heading into a mall-traffic down-cycle. The "cheap" multiple prices current margins, which may halve.
- **Flags I think are benign:** zero debt, a Big-4 auditor and effective ICFR, so fraud risk is low. The 2012 CFO episode was a disclosure issue, not accounting.
- **Red flags I keep:**
  1. The fall in sales per square foot during expansion.
  2. Leadership instability during a strategy reset.
  3. Lease obligations far larger than equity.
- **Green flags I distrust:**
  1. ROE of 35%+, which comes from buybacks shrinking equity.
  2. FCF yield, which assumes stable comps.
  3. The 900-store target, set as comps decline.

## 4. Valuation
- At EV ~$525m, the price implies a steady ~$65m EBIT. A 5-point fall in comps with fixed occupancy could take ~$25–30m off EBIT.
- Fair value: $8–20, with a skewed tail.
- A 3x ($46) needs a return to FY12-style economics, which is unlikely in a mall decline.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 = 0.06.** Mall-based specialty apparel turnarounds are rare. It needs a merchandising revival and a re-rating.
- **O2 = 0.40.** High operating leverage to comps, secular mall decline, leadership churn and lease-adjusted leverage. Specialty-retail base rates in this period were poor.
- **O3 = 0.12.** No funded debt, but leases can force Chapter 11 if comps go deeply negative. Elevated for mall retail in this era.

## 6. Decision
**AVOID.** Conviction 3.
