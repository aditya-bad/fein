# D001 — Blind analyst answer (T = early July 2019)

## 1. Recognition
**Recognised as Jagran Prakashan (exclude).** The listed FM-radio subsidiary, the two buybacks and the regional-language daily make it identifiable. I have some memory of what happened after T, so this drill is contaminated. The analysis below uses only the packet, but the probabilities can't be scored cleanly.

## 2. Business at "security-guard depth"
- Revenue is roughly 70%+ advertising (local retail, real estate, education, FMCG, government and political) and the rest circulation. Each copy sells below its newsprint and printing cost, so circulation is a loss leader that buys readership, and readership is what advertisers pay for.
- Profit drivers: ad yield per page, pagination, and newsprint ₹/tonne (the biggest variable cost, imported and USD-linked). Fixed costs are presses, distribution and editorial. Margins compressed from 28% to 23% as revenue stalled (+1%, +3%) and newsprint spiked.
- Where cash goes: low capex (~2–6% of sales), dividends (~30–40% payout), buybacks, and radio licence fees. FCF was ~₹350 cr a year until FY19, when it fell to ~₹145 cr because working capital ballooned.

## 3. Cases and flags
- **Bull:** at ₹3,100 cr it trades at ~9x "normal" FCF (~₹350 cr). Newsprint is mean-reverting, the 2019 election lifts ad spend, buybacks shrink the share count, and the radio stake is listed so its value is visible.
- **Bear:** print ad growth is low single digits and falling as digital takes share. FY15–19 revenue CAGR was only ~7%, and ~1–3% in the last two years. EBITDA fell three years running. This looks like a melting ice cube priced as one, but the melt may speed up.
- **Variant perception:** the market prices this as a value trap. My guess is that's roughly right. The cheapness reflects terminal decline, not a mispricing.
- **Red flags I keep:**
  1. Debtor days rose 75 → 98. In a business that sells ads to local advertisers this is a real deterioration (weaker advertisers, or government dues).
  2. Borrowings rose ₹150 → 405 cr in FY19 while the company bought back shares. It is partly borrowing to return cash.
  3. ROCE fell every year from 24% to 17%.
- **Flags I think are benign:**
  1. Inventory days jumped 36 → 84. This is most likely a newsprint stockpile bought when prices were high, which is a timing issue rather than a sign of channel stuffing.
  2. The FY16 jump in net worth and fixed assets is the radio merger plus Ind AS adoption, which is explained.
- **Green flags I distrust:** the "3% dividend yield + buybacks" story. Payouts are only safe while FCF holds up, and FY19 FCF covered dividends plus the buyback only thinly.

## 4. Valuation
- Price at T implies roughly −2% to 0% perpetual FCF growth on a normalised ₹300–350 cr FCF at a 12–13% cost of equity.
- On FY19 FCF (₹145 cr) it is not cheap: ~21x.
- Fair value: ₹80–130 a share (₹2,400–3,800 cr). The spread depends on whether FY19 is the new base.
- A 3x needs ~₹9,300 cr of market cap, which requires print to re-rate as a growth asset. That's implausible.

## 5. Probabilities (5y after T)
- **O1 (≥3x) = 0.04.** A secular-decline industry rarely re-rates 3x. Even with every bull point it gets to maybe 1.5x.
- **O2 (≤0.5x) = 0.25.** Already above the base rate. Accelerating ad loss, operating leverage and the rising debt could halve it.
- **O3 (fraud/insolvency) = 0.02.** Low net debt, a family promoter with no pledge and listed assets make this unlikely.

## 6. Decision
**AVOID** (as a multibagger candidate). Conviction 3.
