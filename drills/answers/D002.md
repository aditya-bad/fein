# D002 — Blind analyst answer (T = late Feb 2017)

## 1. Recognition
**Recognised as Celadon Group (exclude).** The June fiscal year, the cross-border network, the tractor-leasing arm and the Dec-2016 leasing JV identify it. I know the post-T outcome, so the drill is contaminated. The analysis below is from packet data only.

## 2. Business
- Truckload economics: revenue per loaded mile × loaded miles, minus driver pay (~35–40% of revenue), fuel, tractor/trailer ownership (depreciation plus lease cost), insurance and maintenance.
- Normal operating ratio is 90–95%. A good carrier makes 7–10% operating margin, a weak one 0–4%.
- Where cash goes: fleet replacement every 3–5 years. Here, on top of that, heavy acquisitions of distressed carriers and fleet growth financed with capital leases.

## 3. Cases and flags
- **Bull:** EV ~$575m on FY16 EBITDA ~$130m is ~4.4x. If the freight market normalises and margins return to 6–7% on ~$1.05bn revenue, operating income is ~$65m. On that, EPS could be ~$1.4 against an $8 share price. The JV de-risks the leasing book and brought in cash.
- **Bear:** the reported earnings look manufactured.
  1. Gains on equipment sales were ~$22–24m in FY15 and FY16, which is 36–42% of operating income.
  2. CFO was −$5m against net income of +$25m in FY16, and receivables explain almost none of the gap.
  3. Revolver churn of $825m drawn and $808m repaid in one year.
  4. In Dec-2016 it sold ~$50m of equipment to its own unconsolidated JV at net book value, while used-tractor prices were falling by double digits. "At NBV" in a falling market means assets were probably moved off the balance sheet above market value.
  5. The JV takes leasing tractors and their debt off the balance sheet, a classic way to hide impairments.
  6. A regional auditor for a $1bn-revenue company with this complexity.
  7. Operating income is now negative, before any writedown of the leased fleet to market value.
- **Variant perception:** the market sees a cyclical trough. I see possible asset-value overstatement that a freight recovery won't fix.
- **Flags I think are benign:** the dilutive equity raise in FY15 (a reasonable way to deleverage), and a regional auditor taken alone (many honest small caps use one). Here, though, it compounds the other flags.
- **Green flags I distrust:** the low EV/EBITDA, because EBITDA includes gains on sale and capital-lease-funded growth. "Asset-light via JV", because it is a related-ish party transaction at book value.

## 4. Valuation
- On a cash basis there are no reliable earnings. CFO over FY14–16 totalled ~$110m against capex plus acquisitions of ~$460m.
- Equity is a call option on a leveraged fleet: ~$345m of debt and leases against ~$225m of market cap.
- Fair value: $0–14, heavily skewed. If book values are overstated by ~$100m, equity is worth ~$4 a share or less.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 = 0.08.** Option-like upside on a freight recovery, but the accounting quality caps it.
- **O2 = 0.45.** Leverage plus negative operating income plus asset-value risk.
- **O3 = 0.30.** CFO/NI divergence, gain-on-sale dependence, sales to an off-balance-sheet JV at NBV and a small auditor match the forensic checklist (memdb insight:forensic-redflag-checklist).

## 6. Decision
**AVOID.** Conviction 4.
