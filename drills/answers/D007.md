# D007 — Blind analyst answer (T = early May 2015)

## 1. Recognition
**Recognised as Ennis Inc (exclude).** Print business forms plus Mexican blank-apparel plants is identifiable. I have partial memory of later events (notably the apparel exit), so the drill is contaminated. The analysis below is from the packet only.

## 2. Business
- **Print:** many small plants doing short-run custom work for independent distributors. Volume is in secular decline (business forms), offset by bolt-on acquisitions of private printers at low multiples (probably 4–6x EBITDA). It is a cash cow: capex is under 1% of sales.
- **Apparel:** a vertically integrated blank-tee maker in Mexico. Its margins depend on cotton prices and a scale war with far larger rivals. It has destroyed ~$117m of value through impairments.
- Where cash goes: dividends (~$18m a year), acquisitions, modest buybacks and debt service.

## 3. Cases and flags
- **Bull:** 11.5x earnings ex-impairment, a 4.5% yield, and EV/EBIT ~8.8x. If the company exits apparel, the remaining print business is ~$380m of sales at mid-teens EBIT margin that is easy to value and high in cash conversion. A sum-of-parts re-rating is possible.
- **Bear:** both segments are ex-growth. Print acquisitions just backfill organic decline, which amounts to buying the melting ice cube. Apparel may need more impairments or a loss-making exit.
- **Variant perception:** this is a value trap with a cash-flow floor. It is cheap, but there's no engine for 3x.
- **Flags I think are benign:**
  1. The big FY15 impairments, which are non-cash recognition of a known bad asset and arguably a step towards exiting it.
  2. Low capex, which is genuinely what this business needs rather than underinvestment.
- **Red flags I keep:** reliance on acquisitions to offset decline, and apparel's reliance on one cotton supplier for ~43% of its cotton/yarn.
- **Green flags I distrust:** "pro forma" EPS ex-impairment of $1.34, because recurring impairments mean the apparel capital was really lost. Also, FY15 CFO of $64m may have benefited from releasing inventory.

## 4. Valuation
- Price implies roughly −2% to 0% perpetual decline of ~$40m FCF (10% FCF yield).
- Fair value: $13–20.
- A 3x ($46, $1.2bn) is not plausible.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 = 0.03.** No growth engine. Even an apparel exit gives maybe a 1.3–1.5x re-rating.
- **O2 = 0.12.** The cash floor, dividend and modest leverage limit downside. A collapse needs a dividend cut plus a fast print decline.
- **O3 = 0.02.** Low leverage, a long history and a clean audit.

## 6. Decision
**AVOID** for multibagger purposes. WATCH as an income holding. Conviction 2.
