# D017 — Blind analyst answer (set 2)

## 1. Recognition declaration
**Not recognised.** It fits the generic profile of several small Indian bulk-drug makers that rallied in the pandemic. I have no specific company in mind and no memory of its later price path, so this is the only clean read in set 2 from my side.

## 2. Business understanding
- It is a commodity-like producer of a few active ingredients sold to formulators in less-regulated markets on tender pricing. Profit depends on the spread over imported intermediates, which come mainly from one dominant country, and on volume in a few molecules.
- EBITDA has ranged from 4% to 17%, and ROCE from 2% to 31%. CFO is positive every year (cumulative CFO ~437 vs PAT ~217, so conversion is good). Cash goes into capex (backward integration and capacity) and into working capital, funded with working-capital debt.

## 3. Bull / bear / variant
- **Bull:** a P/E of 9.6× and EV/EBITDA of 6.4× with ROCE of 27%. The policy tailwind (domestic API and intermediate incentives) and the supply-diversification theme can be verified from outside. Backward integration lowers both input-cost volatility and the risk of depending on one country, which caused the Y-2 loss. If revenue reaches ~1,400 at 14–15% EBITDA in 4–5 years, PAT is ~110–120. At 15×, market cap is ~1,700, about 2.7×.
- **Bear:** Y0 is a pandemic-surge year in one category. If that demand normalises, revenue goes back to ~700 and EBITDA to 9–10%, PAT to ~30–35, and the P/E on that is ~20×. The record shows deep cyclical losses (Y-2). Tender pricing in less-regulated markets is set by competitors from the dominant supplier country. Pharma stocks broadly re-rated at T, and the stock is already up 5.5× from its low.
- **Variant view:** the backward integration may matter more than the surge, because it removes the spread risk that caused the Y-2 loss. Even so, a mid-cycle valuation (~470 on average PAT of ~31 at 15×) is below the current 640.
- **Benign flags:** the equity issued to promoters/investors in Y-6 to Y-4 came with debt/equity falling from 2.7 to 0.8, which suggests it was used to fix the balance sheet rather than to extract value. The volatile inventory days follow raw-material buying around the price spike.
- **Real flags:** product concentration, the dependence on surge demand, and zero institutional ownership.
- **Green flags I distrust:** the ROCE of 27% and the P/E of 9.6×, both on a record year.

## 4. Valuation
On peak earnings the stock is cheap, on mid-cycle earnings it is ~20× and fully priced, and on a successful integration and growth case it is worth ~2.5×. The probability-weighted value is about the current price.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 ≥3×: 0.14.** Slightly above base: low multiple, policy tailwind and integration, offset by a peak-year start.
- **O2 ≤0.5×: 0.30.** The pandemic category normalises and the pharma re-rating unwinds; the stock has a history of 5× round-trips.
- **O3: 0.04.** Cash flows are real and debt is moderate; no governance flags known.

## 6. Decision
**WATCH, conviction 2.** I would want Y1 quarters showing the surge category holding and the integration capex commissioning.
