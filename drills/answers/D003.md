# D003 — Blind analyst answer (T = mid-July 2019)

## 1. Recognition
**Recognised as Gravita India (exclude).** A lead recycler with African plants and this share count is identifiable. I know its later trajectory, so the drill is contaminated. The analysis below is from the packet only.

## 2. Business
- It buys scrap batteries (domestic and imported) at a discount to LME lead, smelts and refines them, and sells to battery makers. The gross spread is LME minus scrap cost, plus a value-add premium for alloys.
- Per tonne, EBITDA is roughly ₹10–20k on a ~₹150k realisation, so margins of 4–9%.
- Profit drivers:
  1. Utilisation of cheap, small plants: capex per tonne is low, so ROCE is high when they run full.
  2. Scrap-sourcing access, the real moat.
  3. Lead-price direction, which creates inventory gains or losses because inventory is held for ~2 months.
- Where cash goes: inventory (~64–80 days) and steady small-plant capex. Debt rose alongside it.

## 3. Cases and flags
- **Bull:**
  1. Formalisation. Battery-waste rules and OEM compliance needs shift volume from an informal market (most of India's recycling) to formal players.
  2. The FY17–18 margin of ~9% shows what the plants can do. If FY19's ~5% is a price-timing trough, normal EPS is ~₹5–7, making the stock ~8–11x normal earnings after a 68% fall.
  3. Diversifying into plastics and aluminium.
  4. The promoter owns a lot of the company with no pledge and no dilution in five years.
- **Bear:** this is a commodity spread business with African political and currency risk and hazardous-materials risk. Q4 FY19 was a loss. Net debt of ~₹245 cr is over 4x trailing EBITDA. The FY18 peak may have come from inventory gains on rising lead. Small-cap metal processors seldom earn a re-rating.
- **Variant perception:** whether the recycler is a cyclical commodity processor or a compounding formalisation and ESG platform. At ~8.6x peak EPS the market has priced the former.
- **Red flags I keep:**
  1. Cumulative FCF FY15–19 was about −₹56 cr while debt rose ₹145 cr.
  2. The ~16-point gap between reported promoter holdings (56% vs 72%) needs reconciling.
  3. Overseas profit share brings consolidation and transfer-pricing opacity.
- **Flags I think are benign:**
  1. Negative FCF in FY17–18, because it funded visible capacity during a 50%+ revenue growth phase. FY19 CFO of ₹89 cr shows working capital releasing as prices fell.
  2. A rising tax rate (8% → 34%), which is the opposite of an earnings-quality warning.
- **Green flags I distrust:** the FY18 ROCE of 23%, which flattered by rising lead prices.

## 4. Valuation
- EV ₹630 cr. At through-cycle EBITDA of ~₹75 cr that is ~8.4x. The price implies little volume growth beyond FY19 levels.
- Fair value: ₹45–120. The low end is a commodity multiple on trough earnings. The high end is a 12x multiple on ₹7–8 normal EPS with volume growth.
- A 3x needs ~₹1,150 cr of market cap, for example ₹12 EPS × 14x. That requires capacity doubling and margins of ~7–8%. Plausible but not base case.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 = 0.22.** Above the base rate: depressed price, a structural formalisation tailwind, a capital-light expansion model and an aligned promoter.
- **O2 = 0.25.** Commodity spread risk, leverage and the possibility that the trough deepens.
- **O3 = 0.06.** Overseas subsidiaries in volatile jurisdictions and a ~4x debt load, but no governance flags.

## 6. Decision
**WATCH.** Conviction 3. Upgrade on evidence of formal-sector share gains and margin recovery in the next 2–3 quarters.
