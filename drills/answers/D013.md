# D013 — Blind analyst answer (set 2)

## 1. Recognition declaration
**Recognised (fairly confident, ~70%) as an Indian anhydride/intermediate chemicals maker with an overseas greenfield plant for food-ingredient acids (I believe Thirumalai Chemicals). Exclude.** The giveaways: products for plastics, coatings, resins and food ingredients; small overseas trading arms; a greenfield overseas downstream plant; retail-heavy ownership. I have only a vague memory of how it turned out.

## 2. Business understanding
- It is a price-taker on the spread between product and raw material: EBITDA margin swings from 3.6% to 22% to 7% to 20%. Volumes are fairly stable, so profit is set by the spread.
- The balance sheet is healthy (debt/equity 0.23), cash conversion is strong (CFO above PAT in most years) and debtor days are low (29). Cash goes into capacity, including the overseas greenfield plant.

## 3. Bull / bear / variant
- **Bull:** it has generated cash through the cycle (positive free cash flow every year). A P/E of 13× at a peak spread with ROCE of 20%, plus a downstream food-ingredient plant, could reduce the cyclicality over time. The promoter stake is creeping up.
- **Bear:** this is the classic cyclical trap of a low P/E at peak margins (insight:cyclical-pe-inversion). Peers also reported record spreads, so the next move is supply-driven compression. A greenfield plant abroad by a mid-sized Indian company carries a high execution risk of delays and cost overruns. Foreign institutions have exited and retail holders have flooded in, which is the late-cycle ownership pattern. The stock has tripled and is back at its old peak.
- **Variant view:** the downstream project could be value-destructive in the medium term, with interest and depreciation arriving before revenue.
- **Benign flags:** the net worth jump in Y-4 is non-cash; the transition to new accounting standards explains it and outsiders can verify that. Payable days are high, but that is normal for buyers of commodity inputs.
- **Real flags:** peak spread and project risk.
- **Green flags I distrust:** the trailing P/E and ROCE.

## 4. Valuation
Mid-cycle PAT averages ~220–250 over Y-5 to Y0. At 12–14×, market cap would be ~2,800–3,300, against 4,600 at T, so the stock is priced near peak-cycle value. A 3× outcome would need a market cap of ~14,000, which means sustained PAT of ~900+ — not plausible without a structural change.

## 5. Probabilities
- **O1 ≥3×: 0.07.** It would need a structural re-rating of a commodity business that is already at peak.
- **O2 ≤0.5×: 0.30.** The spread mean-reverts and project drag arrives; the 5-year price history shows past halvings.
- **O3: 0.03.** Low debt and good cash flow.

## 6. Decision
**AVOID at this price, conviction 3.** I would reconsider at trough spreads with the project commissioned.
