## 3. Business and context known before T
- Business model: collect used lead-acid batteries and scrap (domestic sourcing plus imports), smelt and refine, and sell to battery makers and traders, partly in India and partly exported. Margins depend on the spread between scrap cost and metal price (benchmarked to the LME) and on running plants at high utilisation.
- Strategy statements (FY18–FY19 presentations): grow the share of value-added products (alloys, specialty lead), add plastics and aluminium recycling, add capacity in India and abroad, cut debt, and keep a working-capital-light model.
- Industry context: India's lead recycling is mostly informal. Battery-waste rules require collection but are weakly enforced. Formal recyclers expect to gain as rules tighten and buyers (battery OEMs) look for compliant supply. Lead prices fell in late 2018 and early 2019.
- Risks noted at the time: commodity price volatility, operations in politically and currency-volatile African countries, hazardous-material compliance, working capital swings, and promoter control of a small-cap.
