**The uncomfortable match:** this run-up (2.9× in ~2 years) is almost the same size as 2008–11 (2.8×), which ended in a −45%
fall and a 9-year wait. A Fed that is *hiking* with real yields near 3% is closer to the 1980/2013 setting than to 2008/2020,
when the Fed was cutting.

**Worst case I'd plan for: −16% from here (≈$3,070) as a realistic bad case, −40% (≈$2,100) as a tail that has happened before.**
The bigger risk is not the price but the **time**: after 2011 gold took 9 years to get back.

**In rupees it hurts less** if the rupee keeps weakening (+3–4%/yr historically), but a strong-dollar/Fed-hike phase also tends to
weaken the rupee, so a part of any dollar fall is cushioned, not removed.

## What this means for us (ties to L52 — no prediction needed)
1. **Don't bet on a level.** "$3,500 is the bottom" is a guess; banks cut targets all year. Our tested rule is mechanical:
   a fixed gold share (e.g., 20%) **rebalanced each January** — that automatically buys more after falls like this one,
   without needing to call the bottom.
2. **If adding, stage it:** e.g., three equal tranches spread over 6–9 months (or new money to the underweight asset monthly —
   the SIP rule that passed in L52). Never one lump sum on a "buy zone" call.
3. **Never more than the target share.** The 2011–15 case (−45%, 9 years) is the reason gold is insurance, not the growth engine.
