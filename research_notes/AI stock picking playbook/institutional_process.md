# How Professional Firms Research, Pick Stocks, and Think About Certainty and Risk

Research date: 19 Nov 2026. Sources are mostly secondary (blogs, financial media, firm marketing pages). Academic and primary sources are marked where used. Items not verified in this session are listed under Gaps, never under Cited Findings.

## 1. Fundamental analyst workflow (idea generation, variant perception, models, memos, pre-mortems, alt data)

### Takeaway
Professional fundamental shops run a repeatable pipeline: generate ideas (screens, special situations, filings, deal flow), state an explicit **variant perception** against consensus with a **catalyst**, test it hard (bull/bear debate, pre-mortem, management and channel work, and alternative data at larger funds), then size it. Their edge comes from a disciplined process and from how they size positions. It does not come from certainty. Several popular idea sources (spin-offs, 13F cloning) have weaker or decaying evidence than folklore suggests.

### Cited Findings
**Variant perception (Steinhardt)**
- Steinhardt defined "variant perception" as holding a well-founded view that was meaningfully different from market consensus. It was the core of his process: analysts had to state the consensus view, their variant view, and a **trigger event** for each idea. He held positions "until I feel they are no longer so" (that is, until the disagreement with consensus closed). — [MicroCapClub, "Michael Steinhardt in his own words"](https://microcapclub.com/michael-steinhardt-in-his-own-words/); [Morningstar India](https://www.morningstar.in/posts/35685/banking-on-variant-perception.aspx); [ICICI Pru AMC blog](https://arch.icicipruamc.com/The-Prudent-Insight-Blog/learn-from-the-master--banking-on-'variant-perception'.aspx)
- Steinhardt stressed that being contrarian is easy. The view also has to be well-founded and correct. Steinhardt Partners (1967–1995) reportedly averaged about 24% net a year over 28 years (secondary figure). — [Macro-Ops](https://macro-ops.com/blog/variant-perception-and-how-the-market-is-always-wrong); [MoneyWeek](https://moneyweek.com/444895/the-worlds-greatest-investors-michael-steinhardt)

**Tiger / Tiger Cub process (Julian Robertson)**
- At Tiger, exchanging ideas was required. Bull/bear debates in Robertson's office stress-tested both longs and shorts. Analysts were expected to know their businesses deeply, often through site visits with management and operating staff. — [Behind the Balance Sheet, tribute to Robertson](https://behindthebalancesheet.substack.com/p/a-tribute-to-julian-robertson); [Wall Street Prep, Tiger Cubs](https://wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/tiger-cubs)
- The model was to be long high-quality companies with strong management and short fundamentally weak ones, described as finding "the best 20 stocks to buy and the worst 20 to short." Many Cubs (e.g., Lone Pine/Stephen Mandel, Viking) kept the collaborative pitch-and-debate meetings. — [Wall Street Prep](https://wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/tiger-cubs); [Alex Amirshaw, "Tiger and its cubs"](https://alexamirshaw.substack.com/p/tiger-and-its-cubs)

**Pre-mortems**
- Gary Klein's premortem (HBR, Sept 2007) assumes the project has already failed and asks why. It draws on Mitchell, Russo and Pennington (1989): "prospective hindsight" raised the ability to identify reasons for future outcomes by 30%. Secondary sources often misstate this as a 30% gain in *predicting* outcomes. — [Klein premortem PDF (HBR reprint)](https://wiki.duraspace.org/download/attachments/104563925/ProjectPremortem.pdf?api=v2); [Zeitspace summary](https://zeitspace.com/blog/how-a-premortem-analysis-derisks-a-project-helping-your-team-succeed)
- Klein notes that organizations "really don't like pessimists," so doubts go unspoken unless the process explicitly asks for them. — [Klein premortem PDF](https://wiki.duraspace.org/download/attachments/104563925/ProjectPremortem.pdf?api=v2)

**Special situations: spin-offs**
- Cusatis, Miles and Woolridge (1993) studied 815 spin-offs from 1965–1988. They found significantly positive abnormal returns for the spun-off company, the parent and the combined pair for up to 3 years, attributed largely to takeovers of these firms. — [Academia.edu, "Restructuring through spinoffs"](https://www.academia.edu/87621500/Restructuring_through_spinoffs)
- McConnell, Ozbilgin and Wahal (2001) found the post-spin-off returns are a "shaky basis" for beating the market. A 2007 EFMA paper says later research shows post-spin-off firms do not earn the same long-run abnormal returns. **The spin-off edge popularized by Greenblatt is contested and may have decayed.** — [Purdue / McConnell 2001](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/spinoffs-ex-ante-2001-vol74.pdf); [EFMA 2007 paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2007-Austria/papers/0607.pdf)

**13F / superinvestor tracking**
- Institutions may delay quarter-end 13F disclosure by up to 45 days. The average filer takes about 37 days, and some managers use the delay to hide trades. — [AEA 2017 working paper](https://aeastaging.aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/aiTbZ9QD); [Christoffersen et al. paper](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/media/Lehrstuehle/bwl/Area_Finance/Finance_Area_Seminar/HWS2018/Christoffersen_Paper.pdf)
- Academic results as reported secondhand (I did not open the original papers):
  - Verbeek and Wang (2012) found that replicating hedge funds' top holdings produced significant alpha even after the 45-day lag.
  - Agarwal et al. (2013) found that hedge fund buys beat their sells by about 1.5% per quarter.
  - Aragon, Martin and Shi (2019) found that much of the "superstar" alpha is earned **before** the filing is public.
  — [Arkolith blog](https://arkolith.com/blog/copy-trading-hedge-funds); [InvestorLens](https://www.investorlens.capital/docs/faq/can-filings-predict-performance)
- One commercial backtest reports that 63 of 76 super-investors trail SPY when cloned with the realistic lag. Its methodology is unverified. It attributes the gap partly to 13Fs omitting shorts, bonds, foreign listings and cash. Cloning works best for low-turnover managers. — [HoldLens](https://holdlens.pages.dev/learn/copy-trading-myth/); [InvestorLens blog](https://www.investorlens.capital/blog/2026-10-16-13f-45-day-lag-how-to-use-it)

**Alternative data**
- Hedge funds are the largest buyer segment of alternative data. Estimates of their share include about USD 2.4bn of 2023 revenue, or 55–68% of the market, depending on the research firm. Card and transaction data is the largest data type. — [GM Insights](https://gminsights.com/industry-analysis/alternative-data-market); [Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/hedge-funds-help-drive-growth-global-alternative-data-market-usd6936bn-2028); [Arizton](https://arizton.com/market-reports/alternative-data-market)
- In a fund-manager survey (date unclear), scraped web data and card data rated most insightful. About 25% rated geolocation and satellite data less informative. Vendors include YipitData, M Science, Eagle Alpha and Advan. — [WebProNews summary](https://www.webpronews.com/hedge-funds-are-pouring-billions-into-alternative-data-and-the-spending-surge-is-just-getting-started/)

### Inferences
- The replicable core for a retail investor is: (1) a written memo for every position that states consensus, your variant view, the catalyst, and kill criteria, (2) a pre-mortem, (3) an adversarial bull/bear self-debate (or an AI acting as the bear), and (4) sell when the variant gap closes (Steinhardt's rule).
- The parts retail cannot easily replicate are paid alternative data (often six figures or more per dataset), expert-network calls, corporate access/management meetings, and channel-check teams. Free proxies exist: Google Trends, app-ranking sites, job postings, company websites, and in India the monthly auto/GST/power data, concall transcripts and investor presentations.
- Treat 13F and superinvestor ideas as **idea sources only**, never as conviction. Much of the alpha is gone by the time the filing is public.

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched on expert networks (GLG, Tegus/AlphaSense), how channel checks are done, or the structure of a hedge fund investment memo. Background knowledge only, not verified.
- Lakonishok & Lee (2001) insider-purchase returns: search did not return the paper. Background knowledge says insider *purchases* (especially clustered and by CEO/CFO) carry modest predictive power and sales carry little. This is unverified here.
- 52-week high/low evidence (e.g., George & Hwang 2004, "52-week high and momentum") not searched.
- Joel Greenblatt's "You Can Be a Stock Market Genius" categories (spin-offs, restructurings, rights offerings, merger securities, post-bankruptcy) are background knowledge, not sourced in this session.

## 2. Great investors' frameworks (Druckenmiller, Soros, Marks, Greenblatt, Klarman, Burry, Ackman)

### Takeaway
The common thread is that being right is not the point. Pros focus on payoff asymmetry and sizing (Druckenmiller/Soros), on how their view differs from what is already priced in (Marks's second-level thinking, Steinhardt), and on feedback loops between price and fundamentals (Soros). Systematic edges like the magic formula work *because* they are painful to hold.

### Cited Findings
- **Druckenmiller**, crediting Soros (from Schwager's *The New Market Wizards*): what matters is not whether you are right or wrong, but how much you make when right and how much you lose when wrong. Commentators read this as "sizing, not stock-picking, is where the returns live." — [MoneyWeek](https://moneyweek.com/investments/investment-strategy/605020/stan-druckenmiller-position-size-really-matters); [Acquirer's Multiple, "Greatest lesson I learned from Soros"](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2017/11/stanley-druckenmiller-the-greatest-lesson-i-ever-learned-from-george-soros/); [Morgan Stanley "Hard Lessons" video](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/videos/hard-lessons/duquesne-stan-druckenmiller-iliana-bouzali)
- A related Soros line, "it takes courage to be a pig," means concentrating heavily when conviction and asymmetry are high. — [Livewire](https://www.livewiremarkets.com/wires/soros-it-takes-courage-to-be-a-pig)
- A Marcellus long-read on Druckenmiller (Indian PMS republishing) covers his record of no down year over about 30 years. — [Marcellus](https://marcellus.in/story/long-read-stan-druckenmiller-inside-the-mind-of-a-legendary-investor/); [GHG Invest](https://ghginvest.substack.com/p/7-timeless-investing-lessons-of-stanley)
- **Soros reflexivity** (*The Alchemy of Finance*, 1987): participants' biased perceptions feed back into prices and then into fundamentals. A boom-bust starts when a trend and a misconception reinforce each other, until the gap becomes unsustainable and the loop reverses. Booms are typically long and drawn out; busts are short and sharp, worsened by forced selling. Soros said the theory is not a foolproof predictor, and critics say it is hard to operationalize. — [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/421890/george-soros-theory-of-reflexivity); [Finimize](https://finimize.com/content/how-use-theory-made-george-soros-investing-legend); [Investment Office](https://www.investmentoffice.com/Observations/Capital_Markets/How_George_Soros_Knows_What_he_Knows_Towards_a_General_Theory_of_Reflexivity.html)
- **Howard Marks, second-level thinking** (memo "It's Not Easy," Sept 2015; Chapter 1 of *The Most Important Thing*): first-level thinking says "earnings will fall; sell." Second-level thinking says "earnings will fall less than people expect; the surprise lifts the stock; buy." Key questions are the range of likely outcomes, which outcome you expect, the probability you are right, what consensus thinks, and what the price implies. This is not mere contrarianism. — [Oaktree memo "It's Not Easy"](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2015/09/24/it-s-not-easy); [Oaktree "I Beg to Differ" (2022)](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/i-beg-to-differ.pdf)
- **Greenblatt, magic formula** (high return on capital plus high earnings yield): it reportedly works about 7 of 12 months and about 3 of 4 calendar years. Greenblatt says it keeps working precisely because most investors abandon a strategy after 1–2 years of underperformance and switch to whatever has done well recently. — [Kiplinger](https://kiplinger.com/article/investing/t038-c000-s002-magic-formula-investing.html); [GuruFocus, "Why people don't stick to their strategies"](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/891031/joel-greenblatt-why-people-dont-stick-to-their-strategies); [Acquirer's Multiple](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2017/07/joel-greenblatt-value-investing-doesnt-always-work-which-is-precisely-why-it-works)
- Critics say magic formula returns have been poor since publication in 2005, partly because publication arbitraged it away. Others dispute this. — [Hedge Fund Alpha, "Has the magic formula lost its sparkle"](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/has-the-magic-formula-lost-its-sparkle/); [Stockopedia](https://stockopedia.com/content/why-joel-greenblatts-famous-formula-may-not-have-lost-its-magic-77134)

### Inferences
- For retail, the actionable versions are: (a) size positions by asymmetry and conviction, but cap them (see Section 6), (b) always ask "what does the price already assume?" (expectations investing / reverse DCF), and (c) choose a systematic method you can stick with through a bad 1–3 years. Behavioral staying power is itself the edge.
- Reflexivity explains Indian theme manias (PSU/defence/railway 2023–24). Rising prices brought flows, equity-raising ability and analyst upgrades, which reinforced the narrative. Busts are fast.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session: Klarman (*Margin of Safety*: value as a range, margin of safety as protection against error, absolute-return focus, holding cash); Burry (deep 10-K reading, the 2005–07 CDS trade, contrarian small-cap value); Ackman (concentrated activist book of about 8–12 quality businesses; Valeant loss; 2020 CDS hedge). Background knowledge only.
- Exact 30.8% / 1988–2004 magic formula figure not confirmed (it is from *The Little Book That Beats the Market*).
- Druckenmiller's "liquidity drives markets / earnings don't move the overall market" is widely quoted. Not fetched from a primary source here.

## 3. Quant firms (Renaissance, Two Sigma, AQR): what factors work, and why retail can't replicate HFT

### Takeaway
Academic factor research led by AQR shows that **value, momentum, quality (profitability/safety) and low-beta** premia exist across markets and asset classes. They come with long, painful drawdowns, and their returns are gross of costs. Renaissance's Medallion is an outlier. Its edge relies on scale, data, leverage, execution infrastructure, and a fund closed to outsiders. Retail can harvest slow factors; it cannot compete in high-frequency/short-horizon trading.

### Cited Findings
- **Value and Momentum Everywhere** (Asness, Moskowitz, Pedersen, *Journal of Finance* 2013): value and momentum premia appear in 8 markets/asset classes (US, UK, Europe and Japan stocks; index futures; bonds; FX; commodities). Value and momentum are negatively correlated with each other, so combining them diversifies. Global funding-liquidity risk is a partial driver. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Value-and-Momentum-Everywhere); [NYU PDF](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/ValMomEverywhere.pdf)
- **Quality Minus Junk** (Asness, Frazzini, Pedersen, *Review of Accounting Studies* 2019): going long profitable, growing, safe stocks (low leverage, stable profitability, low credit risk) and short junk earns significant risk-adjusted returns. When quality is expensive, it predicts lower future relative returns. Returns are gross of costs. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2312432); [CBS PDF](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/60211462/lasse_heje_pedersen_et_al_quality_minus_junk_publishersversion.pdf); [CXO Advisory critique](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=23772)
- **Betting Against Beta** (Frazzini and Pedersen, *JFE* 2013): long low-beta (levered to beta 1), short high-beta. It is driven by leverage constraints and loses money when credit constraints tighten. It works within and across industries. — [NBER PDF](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2010/BEf10/Frazzini.pdf); [AQR FAJ](https://www.Aqr.Com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/FAJ-LowRisk-Investing-Without-Industry-Bets.pdf)
- "Size matters if you control your junk": the small-cap premium reappears once you exclude junk. — [Wharton PDF](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Size-Matters-if-You-Control-Your-Junk.pdf)
- **Renaissance Medallion**: Bradford Cornell calculated average annual returns of about 66% gross (after trading costs, before fees) and about 39% net, 1988–2018. He found no "convincing" explanation. Zuckerman cites "ample and cheap leverage." Medallion is closed to outsiders, and RenTec's outside funds are "relatively mundane." A 2024 paper argues these averages should not be labelled CAGRs. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1k2fymby99nj0/Famed-Medallion-Fund-Stretches-Explanation-to-the-Limit-Professor-Claims); [PWL Capital](https://pwlcapital.com/renaissance-technologies-medallion-fund-an-exception-to-the-indexing-rule/); [Institutional Investor, Medallion vs other RenTec funds](https://inv.institutionalinvestor.com/index%2ephp/article/2bswzoz7x91fqht87wvsw/portfolio/the-famed-medallion-fund-is-crushing-it-other-rentech-funds-not-so-much)

### Inferences
- Retail-replicable quant: a rules-based multi-factor screen (quality + value + momentum, excluding junk/high leverage), rebalanced quarterly or semi-annually. In India, NSE factor indices and ETFs (e.g., Nifty200 Momentum 30, Alpha Low-Vol, Quality 30) offer cheap implementation.
- Not replicable: HFT and short-horizon stat-arb need co-location, direct exchange feeds, very low trading costs, leverage, and huge clean datasets. The RenTec example shows that even its own external funds can't reproduce Medallion.

### Gaps
- No source fetched on Two Sigma's process or on the economics of HFT latency and co-location. The search returned nothing reliable.
- No India-specific factor performance study fetched (e.g., IIM Ahmedabad's Agarwal-Taffler-Jacob India factor dataset); worth adding.

## 4. Macro, sector rotation and cycles (India capex/PSU 2020–24, 2003–07)

### Takeaway
Many Indian multibaggers come from cycles more than from stock picking. Government capex and a revival of the private capex/order cycle re-rated capital goods and PSUs from depressed 2020 levels. Valuations then expanded until fund managers turned cautious in 2024. Cycles create multibaggers when you buy at trough earnings and trough multiples, and they destroy capital when you buy at peak multiples.

### Cited Findings
- The BSE Capital Goods index took out its 2007 peak in 2021, 14 years later. That shows how long a cycle-top hangover can last after the 2003–07 boom. — [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/article/markets/bse-capital-goods-index-hits-fresh-52-week-high-l-t-bel-gain-up-to-3-120121400412_1.html); [Business Standard, sharpest rally since March 2020](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/capital-goods-surges-6-records-sharpest-intra-day-rally-since-march-2020-124060300291_1.html)
- The BSE PSU index rose about fivefold from March 2020 to mid-2024. — [Business Standard, PSU giants match Nifty decade returns](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/stock-market-news/marching-in-lockstep-psu-giants-roar-back-match-nifty-s-decade-returns-124062300400_1.html)
- Central government capex rose from about ₹6 lakh crore to ₹9.5 lakh crore (2024-25 interim budget) and was the main engine. Private capex followed only once demand looked sustained. — [DSIJ, Capital Goods: Undervalued Opportunity or Mirage?](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/capital-goods-undervalued-opportunity-or-mirage-53313); [Business Standard, PSU rally drivers](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/what-s-behind-the-rally-in-psu-stocks-and-will-the-uptrend-last-123090500327_1.html)
- 10-year gains to June 2024: Timken India +1,736%, BEL +1,587%, Grindwell Norton +1,282%, Honeywell Automation +1,259%. The BSE Capital Goods index was up 361% over 10 years. — [Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/five-stocks-from-this-sector-rallied-over-1000-in-10-years-praj-industries-v-guard-industries-carborundum-universal-433784-2024-06-19)
- About 33 CPSE stocks became multibaggers in that cycle (promotional source). — [5paisa](https://test.5paisa.com/blog/the-multibagger-wave-bse-cpse-index-generates-33-multibagger-stocks-top-performer-up-over-450); [DSIJ](https://dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/majority-of-cpse-stocks-turn-multibaggers-top-performing-stock-rallies-468-per-cent)
- By 2024 fund managers noted PSU valuations had "meaningfully expanded" and turned cautious. Value Research observed that capital goods valuations tend to hit a high-water mark when infrastructure demand picks up. — [Business India](https://businessindia.co/magazine/psu-shares-back-on-investors-radar); [Value Research, Dec 2024](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/222444/capital-goods-sector-analysis/)

### Inferences
- The professional cycle playbook is: (1) identify where the sector sits in its earnings and multiple cycle (trough multiple on trough earnings = maximum asymmetry), (2) track leading indicators such as order inflows, the government capex budget, capacity utilization (RBI OBICUS), credit growth and commodity prices, and (3) recognize that late-cycle re-rating driven by reflexive flows ends abruptly.
- The 2007 to 2021 capital goods hangover is the base-rate warning: buying a cyclical at peak can mean a decade or more of dead money.

### Gaps
- No post-2024 data fetched on how PSU/capital goods performed in 2025–26 (drawdown size, current valuations). This must be checked against live exchange data.
- No sourced data on interest-rate or liquidity regimes (RBI policy, global liquidity) versus Indian market returns, or on the 2003–07 bull run magnitude.

## 5. Indian institutional sources and research styles (SEBI disclosures, MF portfolios, bulk/block deals, Marcellus/PMS)

### Takeaway
Indian disclosure rules give retail unusually good visibility into institutional behaviour: monthly MF portfolios, quarterly shareholding patterns, and daily bulk/block deals. Indian PMS houses like Marcellus layer forensic accounting filters in front of quality/moat analysis. Meanwhile, most active Indian funds still trail their benchmarks over 5–10 years.

### Cited Findings
- **Bulk deals** (trades above 0.5% of listed shares) are reported by brokers to the exchange after market close. — [EquitiesIndia glossary](https://equitiesindia.com/glossary/bulk-deal-reporting); [EquityResearchIndia](https://www.equityresearchindia.com/post/block-deals-and-bulk-deals-how-to-track-big-institutional-trades-and-what-they-signal)
- **Block deals**: a SEBI circular dated 8 Oct 2025 raised the minimum block deal size from ₹10 cr to ₹25 cr and mandated delivery-based settlement, effective 7 Dec 2025. Sources conflict on whether details are disseminated in real time or after market hours. A block deal shows one trade, not the fund's total position. — [Kotak Securities](https://www.kotaksecurities.com/news/sebi-raises-minimum-block-deal-size-delivery-trade); [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/index.php/news/sebi-revamps-block-deal-norms-sets-rs25-crore-minimum-trade-size); [PolicyCircle](https://www.policycircle.org/policy/sebi-market-reform-bulk-deals/)
- **MF disclosures**: SEBI requires monthly portfolio disclosure on AMC websites. Debt close-ended/interval schemes must disclose by the 3rd working day (2009 circular), and average AUM must be filed with AMFI within 7 working days (2014). The current consolidated rules are in the SEBI Master Circular for Mutual Funds dated 20 March 2026. — [SEBI 2009 circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/mar-2009/circular-portfolio-format-for-debt-oriented-close-ended-and-interval-schemes-plans_14185.html); [Business Standard 2014](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/mfs-to-disclose-average-aum-on-monthly-basis-sebi-114040300039_1.html); [Taxmann on MCR revision](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-revises-monthly-cumulative-report-format-for-mutual-funds/)
- **Marcellus**: the "Consistent Compounders" framework rests on three pillars: competitive advantage (moat), capital allocation, and credible accounting. Its forensic model (based on Schilit's *Financial Shenanigans*) uses about 10–12 ratios to rank accounting quality. Companies are bucketed so that the cleanest sit in the top 3 deciles and suspected manipulators in the bottom 4. A second level of governance checks runs during deep-dives. Marcellus claims a strong link between accounting quality and shareholder returns (firm's own claim). — [Marcellus, Consistent Compounders PMS](https://marcellus.in/portfolio-management-services/marcellus-consistent-compounder-pms); [Marcellus MeritorQ newsletter](https://marcellus.in/newsletter/meritorq-pms/meritorq-forensic-accounting-using-quant-methods-boosts-returns); [Marcellus Jan 2020 PDF](https://marcellus.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Marcellus_Creating_Consistent-Compounders_Jan-2020-1.pdf); [Elearnmarkets, Diamonds in the Dust](https://www.elearnmarkets.com/school/units/diamonds-in-the-dust/marcellus-s-3-level-check-to-detect-accounting-frauds)
- **SPIVA India Year-End 2025**: the official summary says a firm majority of active funds in every category underperformed over the decade to Dec 2025. Large-cap figures differ between sources. Secondary reports give about 74% (3-yr), 84% (5-yr) and 76% (10-yr) underperforming, against the S&P India LargeMidCap benchmark, which some argue distorts the comparison. — [S&P SPIVA India YE2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india-year-end-2025/); [AngelOne](https://www.angelone.in/news/mutual-funds/most-indian-equity-funds-underperform-s-p-indices-over-10-years); [DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/is-mutual-fund-alpha-still-alive-57107)

### Inferences
- Retail workflow: monthly, scan MF portfolio changes (new entries and increases across multiple AMCs), quarterly shareholding patterns (promoter pledge, FII/DII trend), and daily bulk/block deals. Use these as idea and confirmation signals, never as theses.
- Apply a Marcellus-style forensic screen first, before getting excited about growth: CFO/EBITDA, contingent liabilities, related-party transactions, auditor changes, capitalised costs, and the yield on cash.
- SPIVA shows that even well-resourced professionals mostly fail to beat the index after fees. A retail stock picker should run a low-cost index core and keep active bets as a satellite.

### Gaps
- Exact current deadline for monthly MF portfolio disclosure for open-ended equity schemes (commonly cited as within 10 days of month-end; fortnightly for some categories since 2023–24) is **not confirmed** here. Check Master Circular clause on sebi.gov.in.
- Not researched: FII/DII flow data sources (NSDL FPI monitor, NSE provisional daily flows), Ambit "Coffee Can" / Motilal Oswal "QGLP" frameworks and their annual Wealth Creation Studies, and PMS letters (e.g., Marcellus, ASK, Unifi). Background knowledge only: Motilal's QGLP = Quality, Growth, Longevity, Price; Ambit/Marcellus Coffee Can = 10 years of ≥10% revenue growth and ≥15% ROCE.

## 6. Probability and behavior: base rates, Bessembinder, Kelly, thinking in bets, being wrong, sell discipline

### Takeaway
"Almost maximum certainty" does not exist in equities. Most individual stocks lose to T-bills over their lifetimes, and all net wealth creation comes from about 4% of stocks. Pros therefore think in probabilities and expected value. They anchor on base rates, size with fractional-Kelly logic (asymmetry times probability, scaled down for estimation error), and pre-commit to kill criteria.

### Inferences
- How to reframe "certainty" for a retail reader:
  - A good bet has positive expected value: p(win) × upside − p(loss) × downside > 0.
  - Because a few big winners drive most returns (Bessembinder), let winners run and cut thesis-broken losers.
  - Even a 55–60% hit rate is elite.
- Worked sizing example (illustrative arithmetic only): a 60% chance of +50% against a 40% chance of −25% gives EV = 0.6×0.5 − 0.4×0.25 = +20%. Kelly f* = p/a − q/b = 0.6/0.25 − 0.4/0.5 = 1.6, i.e. 160% of capital, which is absurd. That shows why pros use ¼–½ Kelly and hard position caps (e.g., 5–10%), given that the probability estimates themselves are unreliable.
- Sell discipline pros use: (1) the thesis or kill criterion is breached, (2) the variant perception has been priced in (Steinhardt), (3) a better risk/reward exists, and (4) position-size limits are hit. A price drop alone is not a reason, unless the thesis was timing-based.
