# Structural Edges of FIIs, Prop Trading Firms and HFT/Market Makers (India focus), and Why Retail Loses

Notes current to about 19 Nov 2026. The primary SEBI study (Aug 2026) was downloaded and read in full text. Other figures come from press reports, and they are flagged where sources conflict.

## 1. SEBI's July 2025 interim order against Jane Street and developments through 2026

### Takeaway
On 3 July 2025 SEBI barred Jane Street (JS Group) from Indian markets and impounded about ₹4,843.5 crore (~$567m) of alleged unlawful gains. SEBI alleged the group used cash and futures trades in Bank Nifty constituents to move the index in favour of much larger options positions. Over Jan 2023 to Mar 2025 the group's net profit was ₹36,502 crore, so the user's "~₹36,500 crore" is verified. The ban was lifted on about 18 July 2025 after the escrow deposit. As of Nov 2026 there is no final order. Litigation at SAT is over document disclosure, and SAT reserved its order on 21 Oct 2026. I found no ruling after that date.

### Cited Findings
- **Interim order (3 Jul 2025):** SEBI barred JS Group entities from dealing in securities. It impounded ₹4,843 crore, the largest impounding order SEBI has ever made, calling it "prima facie unlawful gains". The order runs to 105 pages. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sebi-bars-jane-street-orders-rs-4844-cr-disgorgement-market-manipulation-125070400081_1.html); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/07/09/sebi-restrains-jane-street-group-from-accessing-securities-market/)
- **Profit breakdown, Jan 2023 to Mar 2025:**
  - Index and stock options gains were about ₹43,289 crore.
  - Losses were ₹7,208 crore in stock futures, ₹191 crore in index futures and ₹288 crore in cash.
  - That nets to ₹36,502 crore. Bank Nifty options alone contributed ₹17,319 crore.
  - One outlet instead reports ₹36,671 crore net, with ₹44,358 crore from index options.
  - Sources: [Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/blog/why-did-sebi-ban-jane-street-from-indian-markets/); [Republic World](https://www.republicworld.com/business/sebi-banned-jane-street-after-rs-36500-cr-windfall-in-india-heres-what-you-need-to-know); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/jane-street-entities-made-over-rs-36000-crore-in-derivatives-trading-sebi)
- **Alleged mechanics, part 1 (intraday "pump then dump"):**
  - In the morning, JS bought large amounts of Bank Nifty constituent stocks and futures, lifting the index.
  - At the same time it built bigger short options exposure, such as selling calls or buying puts.
  - Later in the day it sold the cash and futures positions. The index fell and the options book profited.
  - Source: [Mondaq / Mansukhlal Hiralal](https://www.mondaq.com/india/securities/1648508/sebi-update-%7c-interim-order-against-jane-street-group-for-alleged-index-manipulation); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/sebi-update-interim-order-against-jane-street-group-for-alleged-index-manipulation/)
- **Alleged mechanics, part 2 ("Extended Marking the Close"):** JS traded aggressively in the final hour of expiry days to move the settlement level, for example on 10 Jul 2024. SEBI says JS also used this in Nifty on days in May 2025, after NSE's Feb 2025 caution letter. — [Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/blog/why-did-sebi-ban-jane-street-from-indian-markets/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/sebi-cracks-down-on-jane-street-in-landmark-derivatives-market-case-484348-2025-07-11)
- **Scope of the order:** It covers 18 Bank Nifty days and 3 Nifty expiry days. SEBI said the order "is not a show-cause notice" and that its probe extends to the Nifty 50, Sensex and other indices. — [Republic World](https://www.republicworld.com/business/sebi-clarifies-jane-street-interim-order-is-not-show-cause-probe-expands)
- **Jane Street's response:** It "disputes the findings" of the order. — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/jane-street-entities-made-over-rs-36000-crore-in-derivatives-trading-sebi)
- **Ban lifted:** Trading resumed after the ₹4,843.5 crore deposit into escrow. SEBI told exchanges to monitor JS trades and positions closely until proceedings end. — [Business Today, 19 Jan 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/jane-streets-india-arm-saw-six-fold-surge-in-trading-gains-before-sebi-clampdown-report-511483-2026-01-19)
- **JSI Investment Pvt (India arm), year to Mar 2025, per Bloomberg citing filings:**
  - Net trading gains were ₹4,700 crore (~$517m), against ₹790 crore the year before, about six times higher.
  - After-tax profit was ₹2,840 crore, up 494%.

- **SAT appeal (Sep 2025):** Jane Street appealed to SAT, alleging SEBI bias and withheld documents. These included the Integrated Surveillance Department report and SEBI–NSE correspondence. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/jane-street-files-appeal-in-sat-alleging-sebi-bias-in-probe-125090301189_1.html); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/market-probe-escalates-jane-street-moves-sat-for-full-disclosure-on-sebis-order-on-index-manipulation-allegation)
- **SEBI expanded the probe (Sep 2025),** citing inadequate data and ongoing complaints. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-expanded-probe-jane-street-market-manipulation-125091601192_1.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-ramps-up-jane-street-probe-amid-inadequate-data-ongoing-complaints-125090500792_1.html)
- **SAT hearing (Oct 2026):**
  - SEBI said it had supplied more than 10 GB of data. It said JS should explain the "mode, manner and reasons" for its trades.
  - SEBI said the interim order could "probably be vacated" if JS satisfies it that the trades were not manipulative.
  - SEBI refused to hand over counterparty data.
  - JS argued that some Bank Nifty stock moves ran against its positions.
  - Sources: [Business Today, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/sebi-tells-sat-that-jane-street-has-enough-data-to-respond-to-market-manipulation-charges-559953-2026-10-06); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/jane-street-should-explain-trading-strategies-sebi-tells-sat-in-hearing-126100601048_1.html); [Lapaas Voice](https://lapaasvoice.com/sebi-tells-sat-jane-streets-document-list-is-not-a-short-list-refuses-counterparty-data)
- **SAT reserved its order on 21 Oct 2026** on JS's plea for more documents. This is a procedural plea, not a ruling on the merits. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelawbiz.com/securities-law/sat/securities-appellate-tribunal-reserves-order-jane-street-challenge-sebi-non-disclosure-relevant-documents-555493); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/sat-reserves-order-in-jane-street-document-disclosure-case/story)
- **Market impact:** F&O activity fell by almost 20% after the ban. — [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/investing-guide/futures-and-options/jane-street-sebi-ban-f-and-o-volumes-drop/)

### Inferences
- The case shows the structure of the edge. JS lost money in cash and futures, which acted as the "lever", and made it back many times over in options, which were the "payload". The asymmetry depends on scale: only a firm with balance sheet large enough to move index constituents can do it. Retail option buyers on expiry day were the likely counterparties in aggregate.
- Whatever SEBI finally rules, the economics are clear. A few global firms extracted billions of dollars a year from India's weekly-expiry options market. That is consistent with the SEBI category data in section 2.

### Gaps
- No SAT ruling or final SEBI order was found after 21 Oct 2026. Check for anything later.
- I found no confirmation that a formal show-cause notice was issued in 2026.
- Exact counterparty composition, meaning how much of JS's profit came directly from retail, was not disclosed. SEBI refused to provide counterparty data.

## 2. SEBI data on who profits in F&O (Sept 2024 study, and the Aug 2026 FY25–FY26 update)

### Takeaway
Individuals lose in the aggregate year after year, while prop traders and FPIs collect profits. Almost all of those institutional profits go to algorithmic entities, and they are concentrated in a handful of firms. Losses and profits both sit overwhelmingly in index options near expiry.

**On the user's figures:**
- "₹61,000 crore" is the FY24 sum of prop (₹33,000 crore) plus FPI (₹28,000 crore) gross profits from the Sept 2024 study. Both components are verified.
- In FY25 the figures rose to ₹45,955 crore (prop) and ₹31,085 crore (FPI).
- In FY26 they were ₹44,483 crore and ₹13,896 crore.

### Cited Findings

**Sept 2024 study (FY22–FY24):** [AIR News](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/sebi-study-reveals-individual-traders-face-severe-losses-in-equity-fo-segment); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/sebi-study-exposes-massive-losses-for-individual-f-o-traders-in-india-124092400948_1.html)
- About 93% of individuals lost money, with only 7.2% profitable over the three years.
- Aggregate losses exceeded ₹1.8 lakh crore, with an average of about ₹2 lakh per loss-maker.
- Only about 1% earned more than ₹1 lakh after costs.
- In FY24, prop traders made ₹33,000 crore and FPIs ₹28,000 crore in gross trading profits, "mostly by entities using algorithms".

**Aug 2026 study, "Profitability of Individual Traders in the Equity Derivatives Segment (FY25–FY26)"** (Patankar and Rath, SEBI DEPA-II): [SEBI PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- **Losses and participants:**
  - Aggregate individual net losses were ₹91,685 crore in FY26, down 18% from a revised ₹1.12 lakh crore in FY25.
  - 87.7% of individuals were loss-makers in FY26, against 90.9% in FY25.
  - The average loss per person rose 2%.
  - Active individuals fell 18–20%, from 98.1 lakh to 78.6 lakh in the sample, and new entrants fell about 40% to 20.8 lakh.
- **Options are where the losses are:**
  - Options were 92% of individual losses.
  - Loss incidence was 87.7% in options against 66.0% in futures.
  - 99.3% of individuals traded options at least once, and 93% traded only options.
- **Gross P&L by category (Chart 19 / Table 11), FY25 → FY26:**

  | Category | FY25 (₹ crore) | FY26 (₹ crore) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Prop traders | 45,955 | 44,483 |
  | FPIs | 31,085 | 13,896 |
  | Corporates | 8,092 (chart) / 8,280 (table) | 5,960 |
  | Mutual funds | 5,613 | 2,595 |
  | Partnership firms / LLPs | 4,737 | 2,953 |
  | Individuals | −97,882 | −72,243 |

  The study's prose gives Corporates as ₹8,000 crore in FY26, which conflicts with the 5.96k figure in its own table. FPI profits fell 55% in FY26, while prop profits fell only 3%.
- **Options carry the institutional profit:**
  - Prop traders earned about 98% of their FY26 gross profit from options (₹43.42k of ₹44.48k crore).
  - FPIs earned about 77% from options (₹10.74k crore).
  - In FY25, FPIs made ₹34.41k crore from options and lost ₹3.33k crore on futures.
- **Algorithmic trading:**
  - 99% of the gross profit of FPIs and prop traders came from "algo entities", defined as entities placing at least one algo order a year.
  - In FY26, 372 of 432 FPIs (86%) and 323 of 693 prop traders (47%) used algos.
  - Only 15% of individuals were flagged as algo, which overstates real use because it includes broker auto square-offs.
  - Over FY25–FY26 combined, algo prop made about ₹89,452 crore against about ₹985 crore for non-algo prop, and algo FPIs made about ₹44,689 crore against ₹291 crore for non-algo FPIs.
  - Algo entities grew from FY24 to FY26: FPI algo entities from 306 to 372, prop from 279 to 323, and DII from 33 to 69.
- **Concentration:** The top 10 prop entities earned about 75% of the prop category's gross profit in FY26. They account for 85% of prop options turnover and 65% of prop futures turnover.
- **Expiry-day concentration:**
  - In FY25, 70% of index-options turnover was on expiry day (0DTE), 80% within one day of expiry and 98% within a week.
  - After the SEBI measures these were 59%, 75% and 97% in FY26.
  - Only 3% of turnover was in contracts more than 7 days out.
- **Costs:**
  - Individuals paid about ₹25,000 crore in transaction costs in each of FY25 and FY26, and about ₹1 lakh crore cumulatively over FY22–FY26.
  - STT paid by individuals rose 35% from ₹4,920 crore.
  - STT's share of costs rose from 13% to 27%, while brokerage's share fell from 52% to 44%.
- **Demographics:**
  - Traders under 30 were 43% of the total in FY26, up from 31% in FY22, and 89% of them were loss-makers, against 81% of those over 60.
  - Traders earning under ₹5 lakh a year generated 43% of turnover and 53% of losses, and 88% of them lost.
  - Of 122.6 lakh traders over FY25–FY26, 78% had equity portfolios under ₹1 lakh. They produced 51% of turnover but 70% of losses.
  - The 13% of traders with portfolios under ₹1 lakh and turnover above ₹1 crore accounted for 52% of losses.
  - About 23% of traders accounted for about 90% of losses.
- **Global comparison:**
  - The study benchmarks retail loss rates of 74–89% in EU CFDs (ESMA), about 82% in UK CFDs (FCA) and about 68% in Australian CFDs (ASIC 2026).
  - Brazil: 97% of persistent day traders in index futures lost money.
  - Korea: about 75% of KOSPI200 futures traders lost money.
- **SEBI / Budget measures:**
  - From 20 Nov 2024, only one weekly index expiry is allowed per exchange.
  - Minimum contract size rose to ₹15 lakh, and option premium must be collected upfront.
  - An extra 2% ELM applies to short options on expiry day, and calendar-spread benefit is removed on expiry.
  - Intraday position-limit monitoring was introduced.
  - Sources: [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/explained-how-the-stt-hike-on-equity-futures-and-options-affects-traders-and-investors/article-189260); [PolicyCircle](https://www.policycircle.org/opinion/budget-2026-stt-hike/)
- **Budget 2026 STT hike:** Effective 1 Apr 2026, STT on futures rose from 0.02% to 0.05%, on options premium from 0.10% to 0.15%, and on exercised options from 0.125% to 0.15%. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/markets-fm-hikes-stt-on-fo-trades-surprise-move-126020100888_1.html); [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/stt-hike-on-futures-and-options-new-rates-effective-april-1-2026)

### Inferences
- The F&O market works roughly as a transfer of about ₹70,000–1,00,000 crore a year from individuals to about 700 algo-using institutions. The top 10 prop firms take three-quarters of prop profits.
- After SEBI's curbs, FPI options profits fell 55% while prop profits barely moved. The Jane Street suspension and the lower expiry-day activity hit the FPI channel (JS operated partly as an FPI) more than resident prop desks, which kept their market-making edge.
- Retail's ~₹25,000 crore of annual transaction costs is a guaranteed drag before any adverse selection, and the STT share keeps rising. Even a zero-skill trader loses money with certainty.

### Gaps
- The Sept 2024 study's exact algo-share percentages for FY24 (reportedly about 97% for FPI and 96% for prop) were not verified from the primary document.
- SEBI does not publish firm-level profits. Names come from company filings and Bloomberg/Reuters.

## 3. How market makers and prop/HFT firms make money, and industry economics

### Takeaway
These firms earn mainly from:
- the bid-ask spread on enormous volume;
- the volatility risk premium earned by net option sellers and inventory managers;
- statistical and cross-venue/cross-instrument arbitrage, such as index against constituents and futures against options;
- latency, meaning winning "races" to pick off stale quotes.

Their revenues in 2025 exceeded those of most banks' trading units. Jane Street alone posted $39.6bn of net trading revenue in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Jane Street 2025:**
  - Net trading revenue was a record $39.6bn, against Citadel Securities at about $12.2bn and HRT at about $12.3bn. JPMorgan's trading revenue was $35.8bn.
  - Results were helped by volatility and by marks on private stakes, including Anthropic.
  - Sources: [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/jane-street-outpaces-rivals-with-record-40bn-trading-haul/); [Ground News aggregation](https://ground.news/article/jane-street-396b-revenue-beats-jpmorgan-faces-manipulation-claims)
  - A Bloomberg-reported estimate put Q4 2025 at about $15.5bn, adjusted EBITDA at about $31.2bn, and revenue per employee above $11m across about 3,500 staff. This comes via search summary, so treat it as approximate. — [Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/jane-street-outpaces-rivals-with-record-40bn-trading-haul)
- **Jane Street Q1 2026:** Record trading revenue of $16.1bn, per Reuters sources. — [MarketScreener / Reuters](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/jane-street-rakes-in-record-first-quarter-trading-haul-of-16-1-billion-sources-say-ce7f5bdbdf8af521)
- **Citadel Securities India (FY to Mar 2025):**
  - After-tax profit rose 41%, and revenue rose about 40% to about ₹4,060 crore (~$450m).
  - It runs a team of about 10 in India.
  - Sources: [Samosa Capital](https://samosacapital.beehiiv.com/p/citadel-s-india-profits-surge-even-as-regulators-rein-in-trading-daily-india-briefing); [Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/?p=27351)
- **Other global firms in India:**
  - Optiver turned profitable in its first full year in India. It started in 2024 and planned to grow from about 70 to 100 staff by end-2025.
  - HRT more than doubled its India profits.
  - Global firms (Citadel Securities, Optiver, IMC, HRT, Jane Street and others) expanded in India in 2025, driving a talent rush and exchange upgrades.
  - India accounts for about 60% of global equity-derivatives contract volumes.
  - Sources: [Business Standard / Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/global-trading-giants-expand-in-india-driving-talent-rush-upgrades-125062000121_1.html); [FIA explainer](https://www.fia.org/marketvoice/articles/explainer-meteoric-rise-indias-equity-derivatives-volume)
- **Latency arbitrage evidence (Aquilina, Budish and O'Neill, QJE 2022, on LSE data):**
  - Races happen about once a minute per FTSE 100 stock and last 5–10 microseconds.
  - Races make up about 20% of trading volume, and the top 6 firms win or lose more than 80% of them.
  - Races account for about a third of price impact and effective spread.
  - The latency "tax" is 0.42–0.53 bp of volume. Removing it would cut the cost of liquidity by 17%, and the global equity total is about $5bn a year.
  - Sources: [Harvard Law Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2021/11/05/quantifying-the-high-frequency-trading-arms-race); [FCA Occasional Paper 50](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/occasional-papers/occasional-paper-no-50-quantifying-high-frequency-trading-arms-race-new-methodology)
- **India evidence of the options payload:** 98% of prop profits come from options, and in India institutional profit "comes almost entirely from options". — [SEBI Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Spread and flow capture:** Retail in India buys cheap, short-dated OTM options, and 70% of turnover was 0DTE in FY25. Market makers who sell them collect:
  - the spread;
  - the implied-over-realised volatility premium;
  - the "lottery premium" on far-OTM strikes.
  Because about 700 professional algo entities quote against millions of manual traders, adverse selection runs against retail. Professionals reprice faster than retail can react.
- **Information and risk systems:** Firms see the whole order book across cash, futures and options. They hedge delta in milliseconds and net exposures across thousands of instruments. Retail holds unhedged directional bets.
- **Cost:** Exchange co-location, member-level fees and no brokerage give professionals a per-trade cost a fraction of retail's. Retail costs total about ₹25,000 crore a year.
- **Capital:** Balance sheet size lets a firm warehouse risk and survive tail days. In the Jane Street allegation it also let the firm move the underlying.

### Gaps
- I found no India-specific academic estimate of the volatility risk premium in Nifty/Bank Nifty weekly options. VRP as a profit source is an inference from category P&L, not a measured number here.
- No reliable firm-level market-share data for India options market making was found.

## 4. How FIIs/FPIs decide on India, the 2024–2026 FII selling versus DII buying story, and SIP flows

### Takeaway
FPIs allocate top-down. India's weight in MSCI EM fell from about 19–20% in late 2024 to about 12% by April 2026, as AI-chip-heavy Taiwan and Korea rallied. Combined with a still-high valuation premium, rupee weakness (about 96–97 per USD in Oct 2026) and soft earnings, this produced record outflows:
- about ₹1.66 lakh crore in 2025;
- about ₹2.45 lakh crore by Sept 2026.

Record SIP-fed domestic buying absorbed the selling. SEBI recorded ₹8.5 lakh crore of net DII inflow in FY26, and monthly SIPs were about ₹31–32k crore in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **FPI outflows:**

  - Sources: [Ziromarket](https://www.ziromarket.com/blog/fpi-outflows-india-2026-record); [PL India](https://www.plindia.com/news/foreign-funds-flee-fii-outflows-in-five-months-of-2026-already-exceed-full-year-2025-total/)

### Inferences
- FPI decisions are mostly relative and top-down: index weight, dollar returns after FX, valuation against EM peers, and earnings revisions. Stock-specific merit matters less. Retail investors who read FII selling as a signal about company quality are mostly misreading allocation flows.
- **Structural shift:** Domestic SIP money has become the marginal price-setter. Selling by foreigners no longer produces the crashes of the past. This reduces FII "edge" in timing and supports valuations, but it also means domestic MFs may buy at prices that FPIs judge expensive.

### Gaps
- Most flow, valuation and MSCI figures here come from secondary blogs and aggregators. They were not checked against NSDL, AMFI or MSCI primary releases.

## 5. Fundamental institutional edges and disadvantages, and what retail can borrow

### Takeaway
Institutions have structural advantages in research scale, data, cost, speed and risk systems. In India's F&O market those advantages are decisive and retail cannot copy them. In long-only investing the edges are weaker. Size, benchmark constraints and career risk handicap large managers, and most active funds trail their index over long periods. Retail's real edges are small or illiquid stocks, long holding horizons, no benchmark and no redemption pressure.

### Cited Findings
- **Active fund results (SPIVA India):**
  - Over 10 years to end-2025, about 79% of Indian mid/small-cap funds underperformed their benchmark, per secondary summaries of the scorecard.
  - In H1 2025, mid/small-cap funds beat the S&P India SmallCap. The index fell 0.5%, while funds gained 0.5% on an equal-weighted basis and 1.0% asset-weighted. Short-run outperformance is possible in less efficient segments.
  - Sources: [S&P SPIVA India Mid-Year 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india-mid-year-2025); [SPIVA India Year-End 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india-year-end-2025/); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/mutual-funds/most-indian-equity-funds-underperform-s-p-indices-over-10-years)
- **Speed and algorithms dominate derivatives profit:** 99% of FPI/prop profit came from algo entities, and the top 10 prop firms took 75% of prop profit. — [SEBI Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- **Cost edge:** Retail transaction costs were about ₹25,000 crore a year, with STT now 27% of costs. — [SEBI Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- **Retail loss profile:**
  - Retail losses concentrate in young, low-income traders with small portfolios and very high turnover relative to their capital.
  - The low-capital, high-turnover group (portfolios under ₹1 lakh and turnover above ₹1 crore) is 13% of traders but carries 52% of losses.
  - Source: [SEBI Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)

### Inferences

**What retail cannot borrow:**
- microsecond latency and co-location;
- cross-asset order-flow visibility;
- portfolio-level delta and vega hedging;
- member-level costs;
- the balance sheet to warehouse tail risk or move an index;
- management access, expert networks and alternative data at scale.

**What retail can borrow:**
- **Selling the volatility premium:** Avoid being the systematic buyer of short-dated OTM options, since that is the side that pays the premium. If trading options at all, prefer defined-risk structures, longer tenors and small size. Note that short options carry tail risk that firms hedge and retail usually cannot.
- **Cost discipline:** Lower turnover, because cost is the one certain loss.
- **Position sizing and risk limits:** Pre-defined stop-out and capital-at-risk rules, mimicking prop risk desks.
- **Systematic process:** Rules-based entries over discretionary impulse.
- **Permanent capital:** Retail with no redemptions can hold through drawdowns that force institutions to sell. Forced FPI index-weight selling, as in 2025–26, can create entry points in quality names.

**Where retail has a structural edge:**
- **Small and micro caps:** These are too small for large funds and FPIs to build meaningful positions.
- **Long horizons:** Retail has no quarterly performance or career risk.
- **No benchmark hugging:** Retail can hold concentrated positions.
- **Ignoring flow-driven selling:** Retail can disregard allocation-driven selling unrelated to fundamentals.

These edges apply to investing, not to short-term F&O.
