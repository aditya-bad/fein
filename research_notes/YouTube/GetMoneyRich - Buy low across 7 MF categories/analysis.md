# Analysis: "Buy low across 7 mutual-fund categories" (GetMoneyRich)

Analysed 25-Nov-2026 by Claude. Transcript: `transcript.md` (same folder). Roadmap fit: wealth-allocation / defence layer
(not a stock-selection stage); feeds the planned allocation test (stocks/gold/bonds, rebalanced vs tilted). Research, not advice.

## The claim in one line
Put new money into whichever of 7 categories (large, mid, small, debt, gold, REIT, international) is in its cyclical "bad
phase"; slow it when that category starts outperforming. "A very effective way to time the market."

## What is right
1. **Rebalancing toward what has fallen is real and evidence-backed** — when it is *mechanical* (fixed target weights, fixed
   schedule or bands). Our first-pass India data (25-Nov-2026, unverified): 70/30 Sensex/gold rebalanced each January gave ~13.9%/yr
   with a −39% worst fall vs 100% Sensex 12.4%/yr and −56% (2003–2026, price only).
2. **Directing new money, not switching existing money,** avoids capital-gains tax and exit loads. Smart detail.
3. **The caution in chapter 7 is the most important line:** cyclical underperformance is temporary; governance or business
   failure is permanent. That is our stage-2 filter in one sentence.
4. **Crashes are the best entry for small caps** — consistent with our small-cap drawdown base rates (falls >20% every ~2 yrs).

## What is wrong or missing
1. **It is hindsight, not a rule.** Every example is a cycle we already know the end of. "Underperforming" is never defined
   (vs what? over how long? by how much?). Without a definition you can't test it or follow it — you just feel it.
2. **It fights momentum over the short run.** In India, momentum (buying what has gone up over 3–12 months) has worked
   (knowledge base). Buying the laggard works over ~3–5 years (mean reversion), not 6–12 months. Gold 2013–2019 shows the cost:
   a "cheap" category can stay cheap for years.
3. **Some laggards are structural, not cyclical.** International funds were shut to new money in 2022 by the overseas-investment
   cap; debt funds lost their tax benefit in Apr-2023; REITs carry rate and occupancy risk. "Cheap" ≠ "temporarily cheap".
4. **Rate-hike logic is muddled.** Buying debt *when hikes are rumoured* buys just before bond prices fall. Buying *after* hikes
   (higher yields locked in) is the better version — and that is what 2022–23 actually rewarded.
5. **No numbers for the whole strategy.** No backtest, no worst fall, no comparison with a plain rebalanced split. "Long-term
   returns will substantially be enhanced" is an assertion.
6. **Seven categories is mostly overlap.** Large/mid/small are the same Indian equity risk; the real diversifiers are gold,
   high-quality debt, and (when open) international.

## Our view
- **Keep:** fixed target split + rebalancing (bands or yearly), new money to the underweight category, the governance caution.
- **Reject:** discretionary "observe the cycle" timing without a definition; using 1-year USD gold moves; buying on rumours.
- **Test (fits the planned allocation study):** add "new money to the most-underweight category vs target" as a variant next to
  (a) 100% equity, (b) fixed split rebalanced yearly, (c) valuation-tilted bands. Pre-register; India data; two sources;
  include dividends, debt returns, taxes/exit loads.

Edge check: this is a widely published retail idea — no edge in knowing it. Its value for us is *discipline* (defence), not alpha.
