## Method and caveats
- **Citation counts** come from Semantic Scholar (S2) or Crossref (CR), fetched 2026-11-20/21. Both run well below Google Scholar, often by 2-4x, so read them as relative weight, not absolute counts. OpenAlex was unavailable because the shared IP's daily budget was used up. S2 search was heavily rate-limited, so some counts come from DOI lookups.
- **Effect sizes** come from abstracts, open-access PDFs (the IIMA working paper and the Marisetty-Subrahmanyam NYU archive copy) and well-known published figures. Records whose numbers rest on one paper or summary are marked `single-source`.
- **Coverage check:** before writing, I searched memdb for every topic. These records extend existing ones and do not duplicate them: `insight:us-insider-buying-evidence`, `insight:lazy-prices-filing-changes`, `insight:day-trader-studies`, `insight:iima-factor-premiums-india`, `insight:us-factor-premia-by-decade`, `insight:weak-factor-folklore`, `insight:spiva-india-2025`, `insight:promoter-pledge-group-contagion`, `insight:text-tone-research-summary`, `insight:india-concall-tone-cheap-talk`, `insight:indian-ipo-long-run-returns` and `insight:turnaround-new-ceo` are linked, not repeated.

## What matters most for FEIN (ranked)
1. **Our edge is the inattention premium, not public factors** (#16, #3, #13-15). Published anomalies lose about 58% after publication, and the survivors are crowded. What persists is information that is public but costly to process: filing diffs, concall Q&A, supplier links, late or crowded announcements. Indian small and mid caps are the least-read part of the market, and that is where our reading effort pays.
2. **Process beats intuition** (#35, #36, #32, #33). Mechanical scorecards match or beat experts. Dated probability forecasts with Brier scores make luck and skill visible, and our own ranges are probably too narrow.
3. **Capital-allocation events carry base rates** (#7, #18, #20, #21, #22). Equity issuance, asset sprees and big acquisitions are negative on average. Value-stock buybacks and first dividends are positive. Use these as priors in every thesis.
4. **India governance has academic backing** (#25, #26, #27). Pledging predicts crashes, low-ownership group affiliates leak value, and group IPOs are overpriced on listing day.
5. **Backtest hygiene** (#4, #1). Every rule we test on Indian data needs t>3 or a hold-out sample, and every variant tried has to be counted.

## Gaps and open research for us
- No rigorous, high-citation study of **SEBI PIT insider-trade returns** or **Indian PEAD/accruals** was found. These are good own-research projects using NSE PIT feeds and results dates.
- Indian **F&O** evidence is mainly SEBI's own studies (`insight:sebi-fo-losses`); academic work is thin.
- **Mutual-fund persistence** in India has only small studies; the SPIVA persistence scorecard is the better recurring source.
- Re-check the Fu-Huang-Lin claim that the buyback drift decayed (single-source).
