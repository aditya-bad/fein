### Bajaj Finance 2009–2021: new CEO, consumer-lending engine, ~1,570x
`case_study:bajaj-finance-2009-2021` · tickers: BAJFINANCE · confidence: verified

Split/bonus-adjusted (2016 split+bonus, 2025 4:1 bonus + 1:2 split, ~100x factor) price ₹0.50 (Mar 2009) → ₹786 (Oct 2021) ≈1,570x; ₹1,093 on 19 Nov 2026 (ATH ₹1,141, Jul 2026). Twin engine: trailing P/E 9 → 121 (13x) × implied EPS ~117x. Max drawdown inside the run −61% (Feb–May 2020, COVID moratorium); before the run it had fallen −90% (2006–09). Early signal: Aug 2007 Rajeev Jain hired from AIG; the business was ~90% auto finance; 2008–10 it cut risky segments and pivoted to consumer durables, SME and later 34 products (by 2015); renamed 2010. In FY10–13 profits rose fast while the stock sat at P/E ~7–12 (2011 year-end 6.8). FY15–20: revenue +30–45%/yr with ROE 19–22%. Investors who caught it held through −32% (2013), −31% (2018) and −61% (2020). Ended/stalled: P/E peaked at 121 in Oct 2021; from Oct 2021 to Nov 2026 EPS rose ~4.8x but P/E fell to ~36, so the stock gained only ~39% in 5 years. Lesson: a lender multibagger needs underwriting proven through a credit cycle and a cheap starting multiple; at 100x+ even 5x EPS growth gives flat returns. NPA 2%→12% in 2009 claim is blog-sourced (unverified).

### Page Industries 2007–2022: Jockey licence compounder, ~197x, then de-rating
`case_study:page-industries-2007-2022` · tickers: PAGEIND · confidence: verified

No splits. First listed week Mar 2007 ₹272 → ATH ₹53,453 (Oct 2022) ≈197x; ₹31,065 on 19 Nov 2026 (−42% from ATH). Twin engine: P/E 26.6 → 81.4 (3.1x) × implied EPS ~64x (TTM EPS ₹14.9 → ₹657); the business did most of the work. Drawdowns: −41% (2008), −38% (2015–16), −52% (Aug 2018–Apr 2020). Early signal: exclusive Jockey licensee for India since 1994 (renewed to 2030); revenue CAGR ~35% FY04–15 with a new product line almost every year; ROCE 50–70% with negligible debt; P/E fell to ~13 at end-2008, so a fast grower was briefly priced like a commodity company. Investors who caught it (2008–2010) mostly just held. Ended/stalled: after the 2022 peak, FY23 trade destocking and weak demand cut TTM EPS from ₹658 to ₹458; EPS recovered to ₹708 by 2026 (above 2022), but P/E fell from ~81 to ~44, so 4 years of EPS growth were erased by de-rating. Sales CAGR slowed to ~11% (FY15–26). Lesson: brand-licence compounders bought at 13–30x make money; at 80x+ the next four years depend entirely on growth not slowing. Licence concentration (Jockey International) is a permanent tail risk.

Building-material leaders deserve a premium, but not one that assumes 25% growth forever.

### Pidilite 2008–2026: Fevicol moat, ~80x with shallow drawdowns
`case_study:pidilite-2008-2026` · tickers: PIDILITIND · confidence: single-source

Split/bonus-adjusted ₹19.8 (Oct 2008) → ₹1,231 (Dec 2021) ≈62x; ₹1,580 on 19 Nov 2026 (≈80x; ATH ₹1,693 Aug 2026). 2008–21 twin engine: P/E 15.7 → 93 (5.9x) × implied EPS ~10.5x, so about 43% of the log-return was rerating. Lesson: a true moat gives shallow drawdowns and steady EPS, but even the best franchise had a P/E ceiling near 90–100 in India; returns after that point track EPS minus de-rating.

### Dixon Technologies 2019–2025: mobile PLI volume at 3–4% margins, ~47x
`case_study:dixon-technologies-2019-2025` · tickers: DIXON · confidence: verified

IPO Sep 2017 at ₹1,766 (≈₹353 after the 2021 5:1 split; first weekly close ₹532). From the Aug 2019 low ₹393 → ATH ₹18,398 (Jan 2025) ≈47x; P/E 39.5 → 197 (5x) × implied EPS ~9.4x. ₹13,054 on 19 Nov 2026 (−29%; low ₹9,920 Apr 2026, −46%). Drawdowns: −54% (2017–19), −52% (Nov 2021–Feb 2023). Early signal: Oct 2020 the subsidiary was approved under the mobile-phone PLI scheme, when phones were only ~12% of revenue; Dec 2020 a Motorola manufacturing agreement, then Nokia, later Samsung/Xiaomi/Vivo work. Revenue ₹4,400 cr (FY21) → ₹38,860 cr (FY25) → ₹48,873 cr (FY26); OPM stayed only 3–5%, but ROCE was 25–40% because the business is asset-light.
