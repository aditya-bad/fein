## Case studies

### Monster Beverage (Hansen Natural): best US stock of 30 years
`case_study:monster-beverage-2002-2024` · tickers US-MNST · confidence: verified

Hansen's (1935 juice co.) went bankrupt 1988; Rodney Sacks and Hilton Schlosberg bought it and listed it 1990 at pennies. 1990s energy drinks went nowhere; the Monster brand launched 2002 and the company took off. CNBC: ~+200,000% from Feb 1994 to Feb 2024 ($1k -> ~$2m; $55.02 close Feb 2024); ~42% CAGR over the 20 years to 2023 (BusinessTech). Splits 2:1 2005, 4:1 2006, 2:1 2012, 3:1 2016, 2:1 2023 (96x cumulative): a ~$5 2003 Hansen share is ~$0.05 today [split math mine; 2003 price unverified]. Max drawdown: roughly -70% in 2007-08 [unverified]. Growth was overwhelmingly sales/EPS (31 consecutive years of sales growth); P/E rose from low-teens to 30-40x [unverified]. EARLY SIGNAL visible 2003-05: a tiny company with a new brand growing sales >50%/yr, ~50% gross margins, fully outsourced production (co-packers) and distribution through beer distributors (capital-light), plus action-sports marketing creating a youth category. Coca-Cola's 16.7% stake for >$2bn (2014) and 2015 distribution swap were late confirmation, not the signal. Who caught it: the insiders (Sacks/Schlosberg, large owners); no well-documented famous early outside investor found. Stall: growth slowed to low-teens by 2023-24; stock range-bound ~2021-24. LESSON: a boring-looking small consumer company that creates a category, outsources the capital, and keeps the same owner-managers can compound for decades; the obvious-in-hindsight signal was unit growth + gross margin + zero capex, not a famous holder.

- **Start → end:** ~$0.05 split-adj (2003) [unverified] → $55.02 (Feb 2024) (~+200,000% from 1994 (CNBC); ~1,000x from 2003 [est])
- **EPS vs rerating:** mostly EPS/sales growth; P/E low-teens -> 30-40x [unverified]
- **Max drawdown:** ~-70% 2007-08 [unverified]
- **Early signal:** new brand >50% sales growth, ~50% GM, outsourced bottling + beer-distributor network
- **Who caught it:** founder-owners Sacks/Schlosberg; Coca-Cola 2014 (late)
- **Ended/stalled:** growth slowed 2022-24, stock range-bound
- **Lesson:** category creation + capital-light + owner-managers
- **Sources:** [1](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/02/17/monster-energy-drink-stock-is-best-performer-of-the-last-30-years.html) · [2](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/682867/monster-growth-over-two-decades-turning-r1000-into-r3-1-million/) · [3](https://www.morningstar.com.au/personal-finance/seven-lessons-from-one-of-the-best-stocks-in-history)

### Netflix 2002-2025: two -75% crashes inside a ~1,000x compounder
`case_study:netflix-2002-2025` · tickers US-NFLX · confidence: verified

IPO May 2002 at $15. Splits 2:1 (2004), 7:1 (2015), 10:1 (Nov 17 2025) = 140x, so IPO ~$0.11 on today's basis. Crash 1 (Qwikster): July 2011 peak ~$305 pre-2015-split (~$4.35 today) -> Sept 2012 low ~$53 (~$0.76), about -80%; AP: -75% from mid-July to Dec 2011, $12bn wiped, ~800k-2m subs lost after a ~60% price hike plus the plan to split DVD into 'Qwikster' (reversed in ~3 weeks). Crash 2: Nov 2021 ~$701 (~$70) -> May 2022 ~$163 (~$16), -76%, after the first subscriber loss in a decade. Then password-sharing crackdown + ad tier (ads >50% of signups where offered; ad revenue doubled 2024) -> >300m subs end-2024, stock >$1,000 for most of 2025 (~$110-134 post-split). IPO->2025 peak ~1,000x+; 2012 trough->2025 ~150x+. Rerating split: 2012-21 mostly revenue growth on very high P/E; 2022-25 mostly EPS growth (operating margin ~20% -> ~30%) [margins unverified]. EARLY SIGNAL at each trough: streaming hours and international sub adds kept rising while the stock collapsed; the error was self-inflicted pricing/communication, not demand. Who caught it: Carl Icahn disclosed ~10% in Oct 2012 near the low and made >$1bn [from memory, unverified]. LESSON: when a structurally winning platform crashes on a fixable management error, the drawdown is the opportunity; you must be able to sit through -75% twice.

- **Start → end:** $15 IPO = ~$0.11 split-adj → ~$110-134 (2025, post 10:1) (~1,000x IPO->2025; ~150x from 2012 low)
- **EPS vs rerating:** 2022-25 mostly EPS; P/E compressed in 2022 then partially re-expanded
- **Max drawdown:** ~-80% (2011-12), -76% (2021-22)
- **Early signal:** engagement/sub growth intact during crashes; errors fixable
- **Who caught it:** Carl Icahn 2012 [unverified]
- **Ended/stalled:** not ended as of 2025; post-split range
- **Lesson:** self-inflicted crashes in a winning platform are buyable; survive the drawdown
- **Sources:** [1](https://cases.som.yale.edu/netflix-and-qwikster) · [2](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2011/oct/25/netflix-shares-tank-amid-backlash-and-defections/) · [3](https://techcrunch.com/2011/10/24/reed-hastings-qwikster-symbol-not-listening) · [4](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/highly-anticipated-stock-split-will-take-effect-nov-17-heres-what-investors-need-know) · [5](https://finviz.com/news/230909/this-massive-streaming-stock-just-announced-a-10-for-1-stock-split-the-stock-is-up-26-this-year-and-wall-street-thinks-there-is-more-room-to-run)

### Apple 2003-2012: iPod/iPhone, ~110x with P/E compression
`case_study:apple-2003-2012` · tickers US-AAPL · confidence: single-source

Split-adjusted to today's basis (2005 2:1, 2014 7:1, 2020 4:1 = 56x): April 2003 low ~$0.19-0.23 (Statmuse; pre-split ~$13) -> Sept 2012 peak $705 (= $100.72 after 2014 split, ~$25.2 today). ~110x in 9.5 years. In 2003 Apple's market cap (~$5bn) was close to its cash (~$4bn+) [unverified], so the business was priced near zero. Split: this was ALL earnings growth; net income went from ~$69m (FY03) to ~$41.7bn (FY12) while P/E COMPRESSED to ~12-15x by 2012 [figures from memory, unverified]. Max drawdown: Dec 2007 ~$202 -> Jan 2009 ~$78 pre-split (~-61%). EARLY SIGNAL visible in 2003: iTunes Store launch (April 2003) plus iPod for Windows made the iPod a mass product; iPod units and Mac 'halo' gains were reported quarterly; EV ~0. Then the iPhone (2007) and App Store (2008) created platform economics. Who caught it: widely chased by growth funds; no single famous value investor documented here. Ended/stalled: peak Sept 2012, then -44% to ~$385 (Apr 2013) on margin/iPhone 5 fears and a long dead-money spell to 2016. LESSON: a turnaround (Jobs, 1997) plus a new product category can compound EPS 100x+; when the P/E falls while EPS explodes, the multiple was a depressant, not a driver, and the stock still returns 100x.

- **Start → end:** ~$0.19-0.23 split-adj (Apr 2003) → ~$25.2 split-adj ($705 nominal, Sep 2012) (~110x)
- **EPS vs rerating:** ~100% EPS; P/E compressed
- **Max drawdown:** ~-61% (2007-09)
- **Early signal:** iTunes Store + iPod on Windows 2003; market cap ~ cash
- **Who caught it:** growth funds broadly [no documented single name]
- **Ended/stalled:** Sep 2012 peak, -44% by Apr 2013
- **Lesson:** EPS can carry 100x even with multiple compression
- **Sources:** [1](https://www.macrumors.com/2014/06/09/apple-stock-7-1-split) · [2](https://qz.com/218587/the-last-time-apple-split-its-stock-it-was-a-completely-different-company) · [3](https://statmuse.com/money/ask/apple-stock-monthly-price-in-2003) · [4](https://www.crossingwallstreet.com/archives/2012/08/apples-gained-1-a-week-on-average-for-10-years.html)

### Apple 2016-2023: Berkshire's ~$31bn bet, ~8x as hardware rerated to franchise
`case_study:apple-2016-2023-buffett` · tickers US-AAPL · confidence: verified

Berkshire (Combs/Weschler first, then Buffett) started buying in Q1 2016 when Apple traded ~$90-110 pre-2020-split (~$23-27 today) at ~10-11x earnings, ~9x ex-cash, priced as a peaking hardware cyclical after the first iPhone unit decline. Cost: ~$31.1bn for 908m shares (~$34/share avg) per Berkshire. End-2023 ~$198: ~8x from 2016 low, stake worth ~$170bn+. Split [my arithmetic, approx]: EPS ~$2.08 (FY16, adj) -> ~$6.13 (FY23) ≈ 2.9x, helped by ~40% share-count reduction via buybacks; P/E ~11x -> ~30x ≈ 2.8x. So roughly half EPS, half rerating. Drawdowns: Oct 2018-Jan 2019 ~-39%; COVID 2020 ~-31%; 2022 ~-31%. EARLY SIGNAL in 2016: very high retention/upgrade loyalty, Services revenue growing ~20%+ on an installed base >1bn devices, net cash ~$150bn+ funding buybacks, at a market-level multiple. Who caught it: Warren Buffett / Berkshire (documented 13F, letters). Ended: Berkshire sold ~13% in Q1 2024 and ~390m shares in Q2 2024 (~2/3 of stake by late 2024) as P/E reached ~30x with EPS growth single-digit. LESSON: the biggest gain often comes from the market reclassifying a business (cyclical hardware -> consumer franchise/Services toll); once rerated, future returns fall to EPS growth and the same investor may rationally sell.

- **Start → end:** ~$23-27 split-adj (2016) → ~$198 (Dec 2023) (~8x)
- **EPS vs rerating:** ~50/50: EPS x2.9 (buybacks), P/E x2.8
- **Max drawdown:** -39% (2018-19)
- **Early signal:** 10x P/E, Services growth, installed base, buyback capacity
- **Who caught it:** Berkshire Hathaway (Buffett, Combs, Weschler)
- **Ended/stalled:** Berkshire sold ~2/3 in 2024 at ~30x P/E
- **Lesson:** reclassification rerating; sell discipline when multiple is full
- **Sources:** [1](https://fortune.com/2024/08/03/warren-buffett-berkshire-hathaway-q2-earnings-report-stock-sales-apple-stake/) · [2](https://fortune.com/2024/05/04/warren-buffett-berkshire-hathaway-brkb-brka-q1-earnings-apple-stock-sale/) · [3](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2024/02/15/warren-buffetts-berkshire-hathaway-reduces-its-stake-in-apple) · [4](https://appleinsider.com/articles/24/08/03/berkshire-hathaway-unloaded-more-than-half-of-its-apple-stock-last-quarter)

### Microsoft 2014-2025: Nadella turnaround, $36 -> ~$500+
`case_study:microsoft-nadella-2014-2025` · tickers US-MSFT · confidence: verified

Satya Nadella became CEO Feb 4 2014; stock closed $36.35 that day; market cap ~$300bn; P/E ~13-15x. 'Mobile first, cloud first': Office on iPad (Mar 2014), abandoned the Nokia phone bet (~$7.6bn write-down 2015) [from memory], open-sourced/partnered (Linux on Azure), shifted Office to Office 365 subscriptions. Target of $20bn commercial cloud run-rate by FY18 (from $6.3bn) was beaten in 2017. Market cap >$2tn by 2021, >$3tn by 2024-25; Fool: >1,160% gain since Feb 2014 (2025). Peak ~$555 mid-2025 [unverified] = ~15x plus dividends. Split [approx, my calc]: EPS FY14 ~$2.63 -> FY25 ~$13.6 (~5x); P/E ~14x -> ~35x (~2.5x). Max drawdown: ~-37% in 2022 ($349 -> ~$213). EARLY SIGNAL visible 2014-15: Azure growing >100% y/y, Office 365 consumer/commercial seat growth, deferred revenue rising, a CEO publicly killing the Windows-first culture — while the stock still priced as a declining PC monopoly. Who caught it: ValueAct Capital (Jeff Ubben) built a ~$2bn stake in 2013 and got a board seat (Mason Morfit, 2014) [from memory, unverified in this pass]. Ended/stalled: AI capex surge (2025-26) raised questions about returns; 2026 status not verified here. LESSON: an insider CEO who reallocates capital away from the legacy cash cow toward a recurring-revenue platform can turn a 'value trap' into a 10x+ via both EPS and multiple.

- **Start → end:** $36.35 (Feb 4 2014) → ~$500-555 (2025) [peak unverified] (~14-15x (+divs))
- **EPS vs rerating:** EPS ~5x, P/E ~2.5x

- **Who caught it:** ValueAct / Jeff Ubben [unverified]
- **Ended/stalled:** not clearly ended; AI-capex scrutiny 2025-26
- **Lesson:** new CEO + recurring revenue pivot = EPS + rerating

### AMD 2014-2025: Lisa Su turnaround, ~$1.6 -> ~$200+
`case_study:amd-2014-2025` · tickers US-AMD · confidence: verified

Lisa Su became CEO Oct 2014 with AMD near insolvency: ~$2.5bn debt, market cap <$2bn, stock just over $3 on her first day, falling to ~$1.61 (2015-16 low). Plan: exit low-margin businesses, bet the company on one high-performance core (Zen), use console semi-custom chips (PS4/Xbox One) for cash. Zen/Ryzen launched 2017 (+52% IPC claimed); EPYC servers 2017; Zen 3 (2020) took the single-thread lead from Intel. Two external helps: TSMC process (GlobalFoundries quit 7nm 2018) and Intel's 10nm delays. Stock tripled by mid-2016, crossed $100 in 2020, peaked ~$227 (Mar 2024) and ~$260s (Oct 2025 after the OpenAI GPU deal) [2025 peak unverified]; ~140x+ from trough. Split: started loss-making, so the move was earnings creation (EPS from negative to ~$3+ adj) plus P/S from ~0.3x to ~8-10x; classic loss-to-profit turnaround. Drawdowns: ~-67% Nov 2021-Oct 2022; also >-50% in 2018 [unverified]. EARLY SIGNAL visible 2016: Zen engineering samples matching Intel Broadwell-E in public demos (Dec 2016); debt restructured; gross margin inching up from ~23% to 30%+ [unverified]; insider-led focus. Who caught it: mostly retail and tech growth funds; no single famous value investor documented. Status: market value ~$675-800bn cited 2026; GPU challenge to Nvidia ongoing. LESSON: an engineer-CEO with a single product bet, an outsourced manufacturing edge and a stumbling incumbent can create a 100x from near-bankruptcy; the first proof was a benchmark, not a quarterly EPS.

- **Start → end:** ~$3 (Oct 2014); low $1.61 (2015-16) → ~$227 (Mar 2024); ~$260s (Oct 2025) [unverified] (~140x from trough)
- **EPS vs rerating:** loss-to-profit; P/S 0.3x -> ~10x
- **Max drawdown:** -67% (2021-22)
- **Early signal:** Zen demos 2016, console cash, debt restructuring, Intel 10nm delays
- **Who caught it:** retail/growth funds [no documented single name]
- **Ended/stalled:** not ended; AI GPU phase 2023-26
- **Lesson:** engineer-CEO + one product bet + incumbent stumble

### Super Micro 2023-2026: AI-server 17x, then -86% on governance

## Cross-case insights

### Turnarounds under a new CEO: the first proof print is the signal
`insight:turnaround-new-ceo` · confidence: verified

Cases: Microsoft (Nadella 2014, $36 -> ~15x), AMD (Su 2014, ~$1.6 -> ~140x), Domino's (Doyle 2010, ~13x under him), Enphase (Kothandaraman 2017, ~400x then -90%), Meta (founder self-reset 2022-23, ~9x), Apple (Jobs 1997, then 2003-12 ~110x). Common pattern: (1) the stock is priced as a declining or dying business — P/E 9-15x or P/S <1x, often near-bankrupt; (2) the new leader publicly kills a sacred cow (Windows-first, low-margin segments, the old recipe, metaverse spending) and names a few measurable targets; (3) a single verifiable proof point appears early — Azure >100% growth, Zen benchmark demos, a +14% same-store-sales quarter, quarter-after-quarter gross-margin gains, an opex cut with a buyback; (4) returns come first from survival/rerating, then from EPS. Failure modes: Enphase shows a real operational turnaround can be overwhelmed by policy/cycle; turnaround investors should still ask whether demand is structural. Process rule for memory: don't buy the announcement; buy (or size up) after the first proof print, when the price usually still reflects the old narrative. Expected drawdowns even after the turn: -35% to -67% (MSFT 2022, AMD 2022).

### Platform economics: take-rate on a growing network beats selling units
`insight:platform-economics` · confidence: verified

Cases: Amazon (~2,500x 1997-2021), Booking (~800x from 2002), MercadoLibre (~130x since IPO), Netflix (~1,000x), Shopify (~100x to 2021), Meta (~9x from trough), Apple 2016-23 (Services toll on installed base), Axon (hardware -> SaaS). Shared mechanics: each additional user/merchant/supplier makes the service more valuable to the others; revenue scales as a take-rate on GMV/bookings/engagement rather than as units; incremental margins are high so EPS lags then explodes. Visible early signals: payments or ads attach rising (Shopify Payments, Mercado Pago TPV outgrowing GMV), a segment growing much faster than the parent (Booking.com inside Priceline, AWS inside Amazon), engagement intact while price collapses (Netflix 2011/2022, Meta 2022). Failure mode: platforms that drift into capital-heavy operations lose their multiple (Shopify logistics 2022) and win it back when they exit (2023 Flexport sale). Platforms still suffer -75% to -94% drawdowns; the edge is distinguishing a broken stock from a broken network.

### Capital-light compounding: long runways, owner-managers, low capex
`insight:capital-light-compounding` · confidence: verified

Cases: Monster (outsourced bottling + distributors; best US stock of 30 years, ~+200,000%), Costco (membership fees ~ most of profit; ~29x since 2009), Constellation Software (~300x via small VMS acquisitions funded by FCF), Domino's (franchise; ~180x from trough), Hermès (scarcity pricing; ~30x), Booking (agency model). Shared traits: high returns on incremental capital, someone else funds the assets (co-packers, franchisees, suppliers via negative working capital, customers via membership fees), long-tenured owner-managers (Sacks/Schlosberg, Munger's influence, Leonard, Dumas family), and EPS growth doing most of the work for a decade or more. Early signals: rising gross margin with flat capex, renewal/retention data, management letters reporting ROIC honestly. Where they stall: (1) key-person exit plus a new threat (Constellation -51% after Leonard left + AI fears); (2) the multiple gets ahead of growth (Costco ~50-60x on high-single-digit growth; Hermès ~50x before a ~-50% fall); (3) growth slows to GDP-plus (Monster 2022-24, Domino's 2022-26). Lesson: these are the best 'hold forever' candidates, but late-stage returns depend on entry multiple; a P/E doubling in the last stretch is a non-repeatable gift.

### Category creation (GLP-1-type): the pioneer does not automatically win
`insight:category-creation` · confidence: verified

Cases: Novo Nordisk (GLP-1 pioneer; Europe's most valuable company June 2024, then -73% to below pre-Wegovy levels by Feb 2026), Eli Lilly (fast follower with a better molecule; first $1tn drugmaker Nov 2025, ~63% of US branded obesity scripts), Monster (created a category and kept it), Celsius (new sub-category, 60x then -73%), Tesla (EVs; 35x then -73%), Nvidia (AI compute; see case_study:nvidia). Pattern: (1) the up-move begins when demand visibly exceeds supply (Wegovy shortages 2021-23, Model 3 waitlists, Pepsi DSD shelf space) — the signal is scarcity; (2) peak valuation tends to coincide with peak scarcity, exactly when competitors and capacity are arriving; (3) the winner is the player with the best head-to-head data/product and manufacturing/distribution scale (Lilly's SURPASS-2 and capacity spend; Monster's distribution system), not the first mover; (4) price normalisation (US government drug-price deals, compounding, EV price cuts) compresses margins at the same time as share loss. Early warning for the incumbent: a public head-to-head trial or benchmark that shows a superior rival, and its own next-gen product underwhelming (CagriSema Dec 2024). Lesson: in a new category, re-underwrite on competitive data every year; scarcity premiums are temporary.

### Peak-hype reversals: the tells that preceded -70% to -90% falls
`insight:peak-hype-reversals` · confidence: verified

Cases and declines: Super Micro (-86%, 2024), Enphase (~-90%, 2022-25), Celsius (-73%, 2024), Tesla (-73%, 2021-23), Shopify (-87%, 2021-22), Palantir (-87% 2021-22; -36% from Nov 2025 peak while growing 85%), Novo (-73%, 2024-26), Constellation (-51%, 2025-26). Recurring tells visible before or at the top: (1) valuation extremes — P/S >30-100x (Palantir 137x; only 7 other software firms ever exceeded 100x sales and all fell >=70%), P/E ~100x (Celsius, Enphase); (2) gross-margin compression while revenue soars (SMCI 17% -> 11% -> 6%); (3) sell-in outrunning sell-through / distributor inventory build (Celsius-Pepsi, Enphase Europe); (4) demand dependent on a subsidy or rule (Enphase: NEM 3.0, 25D credit); (5) governance history (SMCI's 2018 delisting, prior SEC case; auditor resignation as the final alarm); (6) capex-heavy diversification by an asset-light company (Shopify logistics); (7) peak scarcity in a new category (Novo). Note: in most cases the business stayed real — the loss came from price paid and from one fragile assumption. Process rule: when two or more tells co-occur, cut size or require a much lower entry; never average down after an auditor resigns.

### Great businesses crash -75% too: separate the broken stock from the broken business
`insight:great-business-crash` · confidence: verified

Every long-run winner in this library had at least one deep drawdown: Amazon -94% (1999-2001), Booking -99% (2000-02), MercadoLibre ~-88% (2008) and ~-67% (2021-22), Netflix ~-80% (2011-12) and -76% (2021-22), Shopify -87% (2022), Meta -77% (2021-22), Palantir -87% (2021-22), Apple -61% (2007-09) and -44% (2012-13), Microsoft -37% (2022), AMD -67% (2021-22), TSMC -58% (2022), ASML -59% (2021-22). Recovery signal that was visible at the bottom in the cases that recovered: core engagement/usage metrics kept growing (Netflix streaming hours, Meta daily users, MELI payments volume) and the cause was management choices or macro, not loss of the moat; management then made a visible reversal (Netflix dropping Qwikster, Meta's efficiency cuts, Shopify selling logistics). Non-recoveries in this set (SMCI, Enphase, Novo so far) had moat or governance problems instead: commodity margins, subsidy-dependent demand, or a superior competitor. Practical rules: size positions so a -75% move is survivable (see strategy:position-sizing); write down in advance which usage metric would prove the business, not the stock, is broken.

### Chokepoint monopolies: customers co-invest, the market under-prices for years
`insight:chokepoint-monopolies` · confidence: verified

Cases: ASML (only EUV supplier; ~22x 2012-24), TSMC (leading-edge foundry; ADR ~24x to $363 and a $2tn market cap by 2026), Nvidia (AI compute; see case_study:nvidia). Pattern: the moat is built in public over 5-10 years of technical risk. Early signals: customers paying to secure supply (Intel/TSMC/Samsung €3.85bn for 23% of ASML in 2012; hyperscalers pre-paying for capacity), competitors publicly slipping (Intel 10nm delays 2016-20, GlobalFoundries exiting 7nm 2018), and rising gross margin despite heavy capex/depreciation. Why under-priced: cyclicality (each still fell -55-60% in 2022), concentration on few customers, and geopolitics (TSMC's Taiwan discount; ASML's China export limits). Notable misses: the strategic investors sold early (TSMC sold its ASML stake in 2015; Intel and Samsung trimmed from 2016), and Berkshire reportedly bought and quickly sold TSMC in 2022 on geopolitical risk [unverified]. Lesson: monopolies at technological chokepoints can be owned through cycles, but size them so the tail risk (export bans, Taiwan) is survivable; buy in the cyclical drawdowns, not at AI-peak multiples.

### EPS growth vs P/E rerating: which engine drove the multiple decides durability
`insight:rerating-vs-eps` · confidence: single-source

Decomposing the moves [approximate, my arithmetic from public EPS/P/E ranges]: Apple 2003-12 ~110x was ~100% EPS with P/E compression; Apple 2016-23 ~8x was ~half EPS (x2.9, buyback-aided) and half P/E (11x -> 30x); Microsoft 2014-25 ~15x = EPS ~5x x P/E ~2.5x; Costco 2009-26 ~29x = EPS ~7x x P/E ~3.5x; Meta 2022-25 ~9x = EPS ~2.8x x P/E ~3x; Palantir 2022-25 ~35x was mostly multiple (P/S 8.5x -> 137x vs revenue ~2.5x); Tesla 2019-21 ~35x was mostly multiple. Durability followed the mix: EPS-led winners kept compounding or stalled gently; multiple-led winners gave back 36-73% (Palantir, Tesla, Celsius, Enphase) even while still growing. The best risk/reward combos had both engines starting from a depressed multiple (the twin-engine pattern: Apple 2016 at ~10x, Meta 2022 at ~9x, Microsoft 2014 at ~14x). Lesson: estimate what share of the expected return requires the multiple to rise; above ~50% from an already-high multiple, the position is a bet on sentiment and should be sized as such.
