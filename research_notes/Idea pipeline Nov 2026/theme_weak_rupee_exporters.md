# Theme: Weak rupee + China+1 export winners (research date 2026-11-19)

*This is research, not investment advice. Every price below was checked against two sources dated 19 Nov 2026. The financials come from screener.in consolidated pages fetched on 19 Nov 2026 around 3:32 pm IST (standalone is used only where the company has no consolidated view, and this is noted).*

---

## Stage 1: Macro check (is the thesis still current?)

**The thesis holds, but it is less extreme than the brief said. The "₹96–97" figure is out of date.**
- The rupee hit a **record low of about ₹96.5–97.2/USD in May 2026** (sources disagree on the exact low). By **13 Nov 2026 it had recovered to about ₹94.2** (HDFC Sky, Deccan Herald, ThePrint). That is still about 6–7% weaker year on year (it was about ₹88–89 in late 2025). The RBI spent heavily defending it, with reserves falling from about $786bn to $721bn. CY26 FPI outflows were about ₹2.6 lakh cr (Outlook Business). November flows are mixed.
- **India's MSCI EM weight is about 11–12%**, down from about 21% in Sep 2024. India now ranks 4th, behind China, Taiwan and Korea (SSGA June 2026; Bajaj Broking; 5paisa).
- **US tariffs:** the US cut India's tariff from **50% to 18%** in the Feb 2026 interim deal. The US Supreme Court then struck down the IEEPA tariffs on **20 Feb 2026**. A temporary Section-122 global tariff followed, and its status in November is unclear. Importers are now receiving **refunds of IEEPA duties**, and Rolex Rings booked about ₹18 cr of these in Q2 FY27.
- **Second-order catch:** container freight is about **2.5–3x normal** (Rolex Rings management, Q2 FY27 call), and most export contracts do not pass freight through. Part of the currency gain is lost to freight. Exporters with a high value-to-weight ratio, or with services revenue, keep more of the rupee benefit than bulky low-margin goods (textiles, shrimp).
- **The IT sector has been heavily de-rated.** Nifty IT fell about 20–25% in 2026 on fears that AI will disrupt it. This is why several USD-revenue IT mid-caps trade at only 10–13x.

**Thesis in two sentences:** A rupee that stays about 6–7% weaker year on year, combined with US tariffs falling from 50% to 18% or less, structurally helps Indian exporters whose costs are in rupees and whose revenue is in USD or EUR. The cleanest winners are high value-add engineered parts and services with low freight intensity. FPI selling has left the small and mid-cap exporters under-owned, so earnings upgrades can come before the institutional money returns.

- This is a cyclical recovery that also has a real mix shift; it is *not* a brand-new step-change. OPM peaked at 22% in FY22–23, fell to 15% in FY25 during the US/EU ag-equipment downturn, and is now back at 22–24% *while the farm segment is still weak*.

- Further drivers: the India aftermarket is 16.5% of revenue, ₹225 cr of new business awards are ramping (3-point linkage systems and precision machine parts), and FY27 revenue guidance is about +24%.
- The rupee matters here: revenue is overwhelmingly USD/EUR through its US and EU warehousing subsidiaries, while costs are Indian. Global OEMs are shifting sourcing away from China (China+1).

**Bull case**
- FY27 revenue of about ₹1,450 cr (+24% guidance) at 21–22% OPM gives PAT of about ₹205–215 cr.
- FY28 assumes an ag upcycle on top of construction: revenue of about ₹1,750 cr at 22% gives PAT of about ₹260 cr, or EPS of about ₹58.
- A re-rating to 20x gives about ₹1,150, roughly 1.6x in about two years, with dividends on top (FY26 payout was over 100%).
- Multibagger path: an ag-cycle recovery while construction holds would bring a return to FY23's 32% ROCE and EPS above ₹70.

**Risks**
- It is a deep cyclical. The FY24–25 drawdown cut PAT by 57%.
- **Inventory days are about 409 and the CCC is 326.** The warehousing model is working-capital heavy.
- Farm demand is still in a slump.
- Freight costs are high.
- A US tariff reset, or a rupee rebound toward ₹88.
- Customer concentration among large off-highway OEMs.

**Kill criteria**
- OPM below 18% for two consecutive quarters.
- Inventory days above 450 together with CFO below 60% of PAT.
- The promoter selling below 60%, or any pledge appearing.
- FY27 revenue growth below 15%, which would mean the guidance was missed badly.

## Losers to avoid (importers with unhedged costs)

This is a sector-level view; I did not deep-dive individual companies.
- **Oil marketing companies (HPCL / BPCL / IOC):** crude is priced in USD while retail pricing is administered, so they absorb the currency hit.
- **Airlines (InterGlobe / SpiceJet):** both fuel and lease rentals are in USD. A weaker rupee together with high crude hits them twice.
- **Paints and crude-derivative users (Asian Paints, Berger, Indigo Paints, Kansai):** raw materials such as TiO2 and monomers are imported or priced in USD, and competition limits price increases.
- **Edible-oil importers (AWL Agri):** palm and soy oil are imported, and margins are thin.
- **Consumer electronics and durables assemblers with weak pass-through:** imported components and chips. EMS players with contractual pass-through are less affected.
- **Any company with unhedged ECB or USD debt and only rupee revenue.**
- **Partial losers:** low-value bulky exporters (home textiles, shrimp). Freight at 3x and buyer price-downs are taking the rupee gain, as Avanti, Apex, Indo Count and Gokaldas already show.
